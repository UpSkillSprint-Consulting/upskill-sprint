## Questions and explanations

### Question 171

Process Task 1 · Hybrid · Very challenging · single

A hybrid recovery plan combines a planned equipment stream with an iterative software configuration stream. All work can begin at time zero when its predecessors permit; the table gives remaining durations and finish-to-start dependencies with no overlap. The sponsor wants the pilot complete within five weeks and has authorized $30,000 for acceleration. The only acceleration options are reducing data preparation from two weeks to one for $20,000 and reducing equipment delivery from four weeks to three for $15,000; they may be combined. Neither changes quality or other durations. What assessment should the project manager present?

Select ONE answer.

| Activity | Duration and predecessors |
|---|---|
| Data preparation | 2 weeks; none. |
| Configure | 2 weeks; data preparation. |
| Training | 1 week; Configure. |
| Equipment delivery | 4 weeks; none. |
| Validation | 1 week; Configure and equipment delivery. |
| Pilot | 1 week; training and validation. |

- **A.** Accelerating data preparation alone meets five weeks for $20,000, leaving $10,000 unused.
- **B.** Accelerating equipment delivery alone meets five weeks for $15,000, leaving $15,000 unused.
- **C.** Keep both streams unchanged and start validation before equipment arrives to meet five weeks within budget.
- **D.** The five-week plan requires both accelerations at $35,000; seek authorization for the extra $5,000 or agreement on a later finish before committing.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: D.** Without acceleration, configuration and equipment are ready at week 4, training and validation finish at week 5, and the pilot finishes at week 6. Accelerating only one stream leaves the other controlling validation at week 4, so pilot completion remains week 6. With both accelerations, configuration and equipment are ready at week 3, training and validation finish at week 4, and the pilot finishes at week 5. The required $35,000 exceeds current authority by $5,000.

- **A.** Earlier configuration does not move equipment availability, which still holds validation until week 4.
- **B.** Earlier equipment does not move configuration completion, which still holds validation until week 4.
- **C.** The stated predecessor and no-overlap conditions do not permit early validation.
- **D.** This reconciles the linked schedules and funding authority before making a recovery commitment.

**Why this is very challenging:** The candidate must integrate two streams, propagate dependencies through readiness activities, and identify the authority gap in the only five-week option.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.1.6.2: integrate and align project plans, pp. 18–19 (PDF pp. 123–124). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.1.6.8: assess and implement changes, pp. 28–30 (PDF pp. 133–135).

### Question 172

People Task 1 · Agile · Moderate · single

An agile team is recovering a delayed service project. Its original vision emphasized adding channels, but the sponsor and customer representatives have now agreed that reducing the wait for urgent cases is the priority outcome. Some team members continue ranking work by the number of channels delivered. What should the project manager do first?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** Keep the old vision until the project closes so historical planning remains consistent.
- **B.** Re-establish the shared vision around the agreed urgent-case outcome and connect backlog decisions to it.
- **C.** Require the team to maximize both channel count and speed without discussing trade-offs.
- **D.** Ask each developer to choose whichever objective best fits their current work.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B.** The agreed purpose has changed, but the team’s decision frame has not. Updating and communicating the common vision aligns prioritization with the current outcome. Historical records can remain available without keeping an obsolete goal as the active guide.

- **A.** Historical consistency does not justify using a superseded purpose for current decisions.
- **B.** This reconnects the team’s shared direction to the agreed outcome.
- **C.** The team needs a coherent basis for trade-offs, not competing unqualified instructions.
- **D.** Individual selection of different purposes perpetuates the misalignment.

**Why this is moderate:** The scenario explicitly establishes the new agreed outcome, so the candidate should restore alignment around it.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, The Standard for Project Management §3.4: Focus on Value, pp. 40–42 (PDF pp. 63–65). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.1.6.2: integrate and align project plans, pp. 18–19 (PDF pp. 123–124).

### Question 173

Business Environment Task 3 · Predictive · Challenging · multiple

A predictive project is behind schedule. A sponsor proposes removing an inspection to recover two days. The inspection is part of the approved scope and acceptance plan, and the project manager has no delegated authority to remove it. The sponsor has requested an assessment, not issued an authorized change decision. Which TWO actions should the project manager take?

Select TWO answers.

- **A.** Assess the proposed removal’s effects on acceptance, risk, cost, and schedule and submit the proposal through the defined change authority.
- **B.** Remove the inspection immediately because the sponsor suggested the idea.
- **C.** Keep the assessment outside the change record so the team is not distracted by a possible rejection.
- **D.** Continue using the approved requirements until a decision is authorized; then communicate and implement the decision through controlled updates.
- **E.** Ask the inspection team to skip the activity but continue reporting it as complete.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A and D.** A proposed recovery idea is not yet an authorized change. The project manager should develop an integrated impact assessment for the designated authority and preserve the approved requirements until that decision is made. If a change is authorized, controlled information and communications must reflect the actual decision.

- **A.** This supplies the decision maker with the necessary cross-domain effects rather than treating two saved days as the only consideration.
- **B.** The scenario explicitly distinguishes the request for assessment from an authorized decision.
- **C.** An undocumented proposal undermines the traceable decision process.
- **D.** This maintains control before authorization and supports consistent implementation afterward.
- **E.** This would both bypass the requirement and misrepresent completion.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must distinguish sponsorship of an idea from authorization and pair impact assessment with controlled implementation.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.1.6.8: assess and implement changes, pp. 28–30 (PDF pp. 133–135).

### Question 174

Process Task 4 · Predictive · Very challenging · single

A predictive recovery plan needs 60 hours of qualified technical review in the next two weeks. Its senior specialist has 80 hours available, but 40 are committed to other mandatory work that cannot move. A qualified contractor can provide 24 review hours and needs four hours of the senior specialist’s onboarding time. Alternatively, a junior can provide 40 hours of preparation that reduces senior review effort by 20 hours, but requires eight hours of senior mentoring. Only one support option may be used; both are affordable, no overtime is authorized, and all onboarding or mentoring occurs in the same two weeks. Which resource plan is feasible?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** Use the contractor: the senior provides 36 review hours after onboarding, and the contractor provides 24, meeting 60.
- **B.** Use the junior: forty preparation hours remove the full forty-hour senior capacity gap.
- **C.** Use the senior alone: 80 available hours exceed the 60 hours of review required.
- **D.** Use the junior and omit mentoring because preparation is not part of formal review.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A.** The senior initially has 40 hours left after mandatory work. Contractor onboarding reduces this to 36, and 24 contractor hours give 60 hours of qualified review. With the junior, review demand falls from 60 to 40 hours, but eight mentoring hours leave only 32 senior hours for it, producing an eight-hour shortfall. The stated mentoring requirement cannot be ignored.

- **A.** This accounts for both added capacity and the senior time required to obtain it.
- **B.** The junior saves 20 review hours, not 40; mentoring also consumes senior capacity.
- **C.** Forty of the 80 hours are already committed to immovable work.
- **D.** Omitting the required mentoring makes the proposed plan inconsistent with the supplied conditions.

**Why this is very challenging:** The candidate must distinguish preparation from qualified review and account for the capacity consumed by onboarding or mentoring.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.6: acquire resources and lead the team, pp. 83–86 (PDF pp. 188–191). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.1.6.2: integrate and align project plans, pp. 18–19 (PDF pp. 123–124).

### Question 175

People Task 4 · Hybrid · Challenging · single

A hybrid project has two recovery options. Both can meet the revised date, but one transfers recurring manual work to a small regional operations team. That team was not invited to the recovery workshop because it has little budget authority. The sponsor prefers the option with the lower project cost. No decision has been made. What should the project manager do?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** Exclude the regional team because it cannot authorize project funding.
- **B.** Ask the sponsor to decide using project cost alone and inform operations afterward.
- **C.** Engage the regional team to assess workload and service impacts, then include those findings in the recovery decision.
- **D.** Give the regional team unilateral authority to reject every recovery option.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: C.** Affected stakeholders matter even when they have limited formal power. The receiving team can provide evidence about operating capacity and service effects that changes the option’s total value and feasibility. Their participation informs the authorized decision without automatically giving them a veto.

- **A.** Low budget authority does not make a directly affected stakeholder irrelevant.
- **B.** A project-only cost comparison omits the recurring burden created by the option.
- **C.** This brings the affected group’s evidence into the decision before commitment.
- **D.** Meaningful engagement does not by itself transfer decision authority.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must distinguish stakeholder impact from formal influence and include operational consequences in a recovery trade-off.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.5.2.3–2.5.2.6: stakeholder engagement and communication, pp. 72–73 (PDF pp. 177–178). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, The Standard for Project Management §3.4: Focus on Value, pp. 40–42 (PDF pp. 63–65).

### Question 176

Process Task 2 · Agile · Challenging · single

During an agile release review, users raise two requests. The accepted scope says users can cancel a submitted request before processing begins, but testing shows they cannot. Users also ask for a new bulk-cancellation feature that was never included. The team has limited remaining capacity. How should the project manager support the product owner’s scope decision?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** Treat both requests as optional new features because they were raised at the same review.
- **B.** Distinguish the failure to meet the agreed cancellation requirement from the new bulk feature, then assess correction and prioritize the proposed addition transparently.
- **C.** Treat both as mandatory defect corrections because a customer requested them.
- **D.** Mark the cancellation requirement complete and use all capacity on the bulk feature because it serves more requests.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B.** The first finding concerns failure to meet an agreed requirement; the second proposes additional scope. Keeping that distinction visible enables a legitimate decision about correction, priorities, and any authorized changes to commitments. Customer interest in a new feature does not retroactively make it part of the accepted scope.

- **A.** When feedback was raised does not determine whether it concerns existing scope or an addition.
- **B.** This compares each request with the agreed scope before making transparent capacity and priority decisions.
- **C.** A customer request alone does not establish that an unagreed feature is a defect.
- **D.** The stated requirement has not been met and should not be reported as complete.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must separate conformance to agreed scope from an attractive new request under a capacity constraint.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.2: scope and requirements, pp. 39–45 (PDF pp. 144–150).

### Question 177

Business Environment Task 5 · Predictive · Challenging · multiple

A predictive project proposes a second supplier to reduce interruption risk. The risk model assumes the two suppliers fail independently. During review, the team learns that both may depend on the same specialist upstream plant and transport terminal. The dependency is not yet confirmed. Which TWO actions should the project manager take before relying on the proposed risk reduction?

Select TWO answers.

- **A.** Keep the independence assumption because the suppliers have different company names.
- **B.** Verify the shared dependencies and assess how a common disruption could affect both suppliers.
- **C.** Remove all delivery contingency because two contracts provide complete protection.
- **D.** Treat the possibility of a shared dependency as proof that no second supplier can ever reduce risk.
- **E.** Reassess the response and residual exposure using the findings, including genuinely independent alternatives or a contingency if needed.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B and E.** Two contracts do not necessarily create two independent sources of resilience. The project should investigate the possible common cause, assess its effect on combined exposure, and revise the response accordingly. The signal is neither proof of independence nor proof that diversification cannot work.

- **A.** Legal identity does not establish operational independence.
- **B.** This tests the assumption that determines the proposed response’s effectiveness.
- **C.** Residual and common-cause exposure may remain and has not been assessed.
- **D.** The dependency is unconfirmed and alternatives may still provide useful risk reduction.
- **E.** This uses the evidence to adapt the response rather than retaining an unsupported risk model.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must challenge a dependency assumption without overreacting to an unconfirmed signal, then revise residual-risk planning.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.7.2: identify, analyze, and respond to risk, pp. 96–100 (PDF pp. 201–205).

### Question 178

People Task 6 · Agile · Moderate · single

An agile service team agreed with customers that urgent requests would receive a useful first response within four hours. After a pilot, the product’s automated acknowledgement arrives immediately, but customers still wait a day for substantive guidance and report that the outcome has not improved. What should the project manager do?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** Report success because an automatic message was delivered in less than four hours.
- **B.** Change the metric to acknowledgement speed without discussing it with the customer.
- **C.** Explain that customers should lower their expectations now that the product is live.
- **D.** Review the unmet expectation with customers and the responsible service team, agree corrective action, and track time to a useful response.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: D.** The agreement concerns a useful response, not receipt of an automated acknowledgement. The project manager should respond to the evidence of an unmet expectation and work with those responsible for the service to improve the intended outcome. The measurement should preserve the agreed meaning.

- **A.** An acknowledgement is not the substantive response defined in the expectation.
- **B.** Changing the measure unilaterally hides rather than resolves the unmet outcome.
- **C.** Delivery does not justify dismissing an agreed customer expectation.
- **D.** This addresses the actual expectation and keeps the feedback measure aligned with it.

**Why this is moderate:** The candidate applies the clearly stated customer expectation instead of substituting an easier metric.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.5.2.3–2.5.2.6: stakeholder engagement and communication, pp. 72–73 (PDF pp. 177–178). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, The Standard for Project Management §3.4: Focus on Value, pp. 40–42 (PDF pp. 63–65).

### Question 179

Business Environment Task 4 · Predictive · Challenging · single

A predictive project manager must sequence responses to three reported items. A confirmed access fault blocks tomorrow’s mandatory acceptance test; the responsible technician is available only this afternoon. A report-format defect has a verified workaround and is due next week. A supplier warns of a possible delay next month, but no delay has occurred. What should the project manager prioritize first?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** The report-format defect, because it can probably be closed fastest.
- **B.** The possible supplier delay, because every risk should be resolved before an existing issue.
- **C.** Assign and coordinate restoration of test access today, then verify readiness while tracking the other items appropriately.
- **D.** Wait for all three items to become urgent so one coordinated escalation can address them.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: C.** The access fault is an active issue with an imminent dependent activity and a closing resource window. Acting today preserves the acceptance test, and verification ensures that the impediment is actually removed. The workaround reduces the immediate urgency of the format defect; the potential supplier event needs risk tracking rather than being treated as an already realized delay.

- **A.** Ease of closure does not outweigh the stated impact and time window of the blocked acceptance test.
- **B.** Risk and issue priority depends on exposure and urgency; the potential future event does not automatically outrank the current blocker.
- **C.** This addresses the most time-sensitive active impediment and includes verification of resolution.
- **D.** Waiting discards the available recovery window and creates avoidable delay.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must prioritize by actual impact and urgency while distinguishing a current impediment, a controlled defect, and an uncertain event.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide Section 4: issue log, pp. 123 (PDF pp. 228). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide §2.7.2: risk identification and analysis, pp. 96–98 (PDF pp. 201–203).

### Question 180

Process Task 3 · Hybrid · Challenging · single

A hybrid project has already spent $140,000, which cannot be recovered. The sponsor is comparing two feasible release options that both meet mandatory requirements, can launch on the same date, and have equivalent risk. A focused release needs $40,000 more and has forecast future benefits of $70,000. The broader release needs $90,000 more and has forecast future benefits of $100,000. All amounts use the same evaluation horizon and basis; the stated benefits exclude earlier costs. The approved decision rule is to maximize future benefits less remaining cost, and either option is within funding authority. What should the project manager recommend?

Select ONE answer.

- **A.** The focused release, with $30,000 forecast incremental net value compared with $10,000 for the broader release, followed by monitoring of the benefit assumptions.
- **B.** The broader release because $100,000 is the larger gross benefit, regardless of remaining cost.
- **C.** The broader release because the $140,000 already spent must be recovered through the largest possible scope.
- **D.** Reject both options because including the unrecoverable $140,000 makes each remaining option appear negative.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A.** The focused option offers $70,000 of future benefits less $40,000 of remaining cost, or $30,000 incremental net value. The broader option offers $100,000 less $90,000, or $10,000. Under the explicit rule and equal feasibility and risk conditions, the focused release is preferred. The common sunk expenditure does not change this forward-looking comparison. Forecast benefits still need subsequent verification.

- **A.** This follows the stated rule, distinguishes incremental value from past spending, and preserves benefit verification.
- **B.** Gross benefits omit the additional investment required to obtain them.
- **C.** Irrecoverable spending is not a justification for choosing more scope with lower incremental value.
- **D.** Past spending cannot be changed by either option and does not justify rejecting a positive future contribution under the given rule.

**Why this is challenging:** The candidate must apply the explicit value rule while resisting both gross-benefit and sunk-cost reasoning.

**References:** PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, The Standard for Project Management §3.4: Focus on Value, pp. 40–42 (PDF pp. 63–65). PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition, Guide Section 4: benefits management plan and business case, pp. 115–116 (PDF pp. 220–221).
