## Question NPPE-041

**Blueprint:** II.2 · **Difficulty:** easy (provisional) · **Cognition:** understanding

A project mentor praises a junior engineer for showing courage: the engineer raised a substantiated concern respectfully instead of staying silent or making reckless accusations. The mentor emphasizes developing sound character and judgment between two extremes. Which ethical perspective most directly matches that reasoning?

A. Duty ethics, because the mentor says one unqualified rule applies without exception.
B. Utilitarianism, because the mentor calculates aggregate costs and benefits to everyone.
C. Virtue ethics, emphasizing courage as a character trait exercised with judgment.
D. Rights ethics, because the mentor begins with a specific person's legally protected entitlement.

**Answer:** C

**Why:**

- **A:** Duty ethics emphasizes obligations and universal principles, not the mentor's focus on cultivating good character between extremes.
- **B:** No consequences or aggregate benefits are calculated. The decisive clue is a disposition toward courageous but prudent conduct.
- **C — correct:** Aristotelian virtue ethics emphasizes cultivated virtues such as courage, with a reasoned mean between cowardice and recklessness.
- **D:** The mentor does not reason from an individual's entitlement, which would be a rights-based starting point.

**Learning objective:** Recognize character-based virtue ethics, including the reasoned mean between extremes, in professional decisions.

**Key point:** Recognize the reasoning used: character and the balanced exercise of courage point to virtue ethics.

**Common trap:** Confusing ethically desirable conduct with any ethical theory that could also support it.

**Jurisdiction:** Ethical theory applied in Canada; no specific statute is presumed.

**References:**

- Andrews, Shaw and McPhee, Canadian Professional Engineering and Geoscience: Practice and Ethics, 6th ed. (2019) — Ch. 9, section 9.2, printed pp. 210–211: Aristotle’s virtue ethics and the golden mean (*textbook*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — II.2 — Ethical Theories and Principles (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-042

**Blueprint:** II.3 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

A graduate-in-training developed and validated an important analysis used in a licensed engineer's final project report. The supervising engineer checks and accepts responsibility for the conclusions but plans to list only their own contribution, saying the graduate cannot authenticate the report. What is the best response?

A. Acknowledge the trainee's substantive work while retaining responsibility for the reviewed report.
B. Name only licensed reviewers in the report and recognize the trainee solely in internal timesheets.
C. Transfer professional responsibility to the trainee because the original analysis was their work.
D. Postpone acknowledging the trainee's contribution until they become professionally licensed.

**Answer:** A

**Why:**

- **A — correct:** Giving fair credit for work and retaining responsibility for its professional approval are separate matters; both can be respected.
- **B:** Internal timesheets are not a substitute for appropriate acknowledgement of substantial work in the project record; unlicensed status does not prevent fair credit.
- **C:** Contributing the analysis does not automatically confer authorization to take responsibility for authenticated professional conclusions.
- **D:** Attribution should not depend on the contributor's eventual licensure. Credit and professional sign-off perform different functions.

**Learning objective:** Apply ethical duties of fairness and giving credit to colleagues and supervised personnel.

**Key point:** Distinguish ethical recognition of contributors from the licensed person's responsibility to review and authenticate.

**Common trap:** Treating licensure as a prerequisite for attribution, or credit as a transfer of professional accountability.

**Jurisdiction:** Canada; general code-of-ethics principles, subject to each professional regulator's enforceable provisions.

**References:**

- Andrews, Shaw and McPhee, Canadian Professional Engineering and Geoscience: Practice and Ethics, 6th ed. (2019) — Ch. 9, section 9.7, printed pp. 220–221: equitable treatment, fair criticism, and giving due credit (*textbook*)
- [Engineers Canada, Guideline on the code of ethics](https://engineerscanada.ca/guidelines-and-papers/public-guideline-on-the-code-of-ethics) — Principles 7 and 8: acknowledge contributions; responsibility for reviewed and supervised work (*national-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — II.3 — Codes of Ethics of Professional Engineers and Geoscientists in Canada (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-043

**Blueprint:** III.4 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

A technical society publishes a consensus standard that would ordinarily be voluntary. A municipality issues a legally enforceable construction permit expressly requiring that project's design to meet the specified edition. The designer argues that no society standard can be mandatory. Which interpretation is best?

A. The latest edition automatically replaces the specified edition whenever a technical society updates its website.
B. A client can waive compliance with any permit condition by signing a private risk acceptance form.
C. The society's publication remains optional on this project because the society is not a legislature.
D. The requirement can be binding through the permit even though the original society publication was voluntary.

**Answer:** D

**Why:**

- **A:** An updated voluntary publication does not automatically amend a legally effective permit specifying an edition.
- **B:** A private client agreement does not generally override a binding permit requirement imposed by an authority with jurisdiction.
- **C:** The voluntary source of a standard does not settle the legal effect of its incorporation into another binding instrument.
- **D — correct:** The stated permit makes the referenced standard a project obligation; its legal force derives from the governing permit, not the society's independent legislative authority.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish a voluntary technical publication from a requirement made binding by an applicable legal instrument.

**Key point:** Assess the source of legal force: a technical standard may become mandatory when validly incorporated into a binding permit or regulation.

**Common trap:** Assuming a voluntary standards publisher prevents the standard's later incorporation into a binding requirement.

**Jurisdiction:** Hypothetical Canadian municipal project; assumes an applicable permit expressly makes the edition a mandatory condition.

**References:**

- Samuels and Sanders, Practical Law of Architecture, Engineering, and Geoscience, Canadian ed. (2007) — Ch. 13, section 13.5, printed pp. 149–150: legislated, contractual and voluntary standards (*textbook*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — III.4 — Statutory and Non-Statutory Standards and Codes of Practice (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-044

**Blueprint:** III.7 · **Difficulty:** hard (provisional) · **Cognition:** analysis

An engineer emails a confidential geotechnical dataset to a personal webmail account to finish a report at home. The engineer realizes the account has been compromised and that outsiders may have accessed the file. What is the most appropriate initial response?

A. Delete the sent email and assume the risk ends because it is no longer in the engineer's mailbox.
B. Notify the incident-response team promptly, preserve facts and help contain the disclosure.
C. Publish the entire dataset so all potential recipients have equal access to the information.
D. Wait for proof that somebody used the dataset before notifying anyone, to avoid an unnecessary investigation.

**Answer:** B

**Why:**

- **A:** Deleting a local message neither establishes that external copies were removed nor meets the obligation to investigate a suspected incident.
- **B — correct:** Timely internal reporting and coordinated containment permit a proper assessment of scope, confidentiality duties and any applicable client or legal notifications.
- **C:** Public release would magnify the confidentiality breach and is not an appropriate incident-response measure.
- **D:** A suspected compromise can require prompt reporting and containment before the precise extent of access is known.

**Learning objective:** Identify proportionate containment and incident reporting when a professional creates a potential confidentiality or cyber-security breach.

**Key point:** Treat suspected confidential-data compromise as an incident requiring timely reporting, proportionate containment and documented assessment.

**Common trap:** Confusing deletion, secrecy or publication with effective security-incident management.

**Jurisdiction:** Canadian engineering office; assumes an internal incident-response procedure and confidential proprietary project data; whether external breach notification is mandatory must be assessed.

**References:**

- Andrews, Shaw and McPhee, Canadian Professional Engineering and Geoscience: Practice and Ethics, 6th ed. (2019) — Ch. 7, sections 7.4–7.5, printed pp. 166–169: responsible software and computer security practices (*textbook*)
- [Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, Developing your incident response plan (ITSAP.40.003)](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/developing-your-incident-response-plan-itsap40003) — Detection, analysis, stakeholder notification, containment and documentation (*current-government-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — III.7 — Use of Software, Computers and Internet-based Tools; Liability for Software Errors (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-045

**Blueprint:** III.9 · **Difficulty:** hard (provisional) · **Cognition:** analysis

During a routine records check, a professional discovers authenticated audit records showing that a colleague deliberately substituted passing results for failed material tests. The affected components are already installed, but the actual risk has not yet been determined. The manager asks that the matter be handled quietly next quarter. What is the best next course?

A. Preserve the evidence and complete an internal investigation, but postpone any other response until the next reporting cycle.
B. Submit an unqualified misconduct allegation immediately without identifying affected installations or arranging safety assessment.
C. Notify all project owners that every installation is unsafe before determining what the records show about their work.
D. Preserve evidence, assess and control affected work promptly, and report through appropriate channels as required.

**Answer:** D

**Why:**

- **A:** An internal inquiry can be necessary, but delaying action on affected installations and any relevant regulatory duties could leave avoidable risks unresolved.
- **B:** Prompt reporting matters, but an unsupported blanket allegation with no effort to identify affected work is not a proportionate complete response.
- **C:** Risk communication should be accurate and timely; asserting every installation is unsafe without assessing the evidence can mislead stakeholders.
- **D — correct:** Evidence preservation, time-sensitive risk assessment and proportionate reporting together address public protection and professional transparency.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish a reasoned duty to inform responsible authorities from secrecy or indiscriminate public allegations.

**Key point:** A professional's duty to inform is not satisfied by silence or unsupported public claims; use credible evidence and appropriate protective channels.

**Common trap:** Waiting for injury or substituting publicity for evidence-based regulatory and safety escalation.

**Jurisdiction:** Canada; specific regulator-reporting obligations vary by province/territory and require case-specific assessment.

**References:**

- Andrews, Shaw and McPhee, Canadian Professional Engineering and Geoscience: Practice and Ethics, 6th ed. (2019) — Ch. 10, professional duties to employers and reporting unsafe or unethical decisions, printed pp. 231–239 (*textbook*)
- [Engineers Canada, Guideline on the duty to report](https://engineerscanada.ca/guidelines-and-papers/guideline-on-the-duty-to-report) — Reporting where unethical or illegal conduct may pose a risk; detailed duties vary by regulator (*national-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — III.9 — Duty to Inform; Whistleblowing (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-046

**Blueprint:** IV.5 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

A firm's contract calls a designer an 'independent contractor.' In practice, the firm controls daily hours and work methods, supplies all design equipment, forbids other clients and pays a fixed wage with no opportunity for profit or risk of loss. Which conclusion is most defensible?

A. The facts suggest employment, but the total working relationship must be assessed beyond the contract label.
B. Use only the stated contract intention, because invoicing the firm is definitive evidence of self-employment.
C. Treat professional discretion as the decisive factor, because the firm cannot direct a licensed designer's work.
D. Classify the worker by who purchases professional liability insurance rather than by how their work is performed.

**Answer:** A

**Why:**

- **A — correct:** Control, supplied equipment, economic dependence and absence of entrepreneurial opportunity tend toward employment; a complete factual assessment is still required.
- **B:** Parties' intention and invoicing arrangements are relevant but not conclusive where the working realities differ from the label.
- **C:** Professional skill or discretion does not necessarily mean the person operates a genuinely independent business.
- **D:** Liability insurance may be relevant commercially, but does not supersede the broader statutory or common-law employment-status assessment.

**Learning objective:** Apply the factual total-relationship approach to distinguishing employees from independent contractors.

**Key point:** Look at the substance of the relationship—control, tools, profit opportunity and risk—not just the title of the contract.

**Common trap:** Treating the signed 'contractor' label or professional status as determinative.

**Jurisdiction:** Canadian common-law province (outside Quebec); this is a general classification principle, not a final tax or labour ruling.

**References:**

- Samuels and Sanders, Practical Law of Architecture, Engineering, and Geoscience, Canadian ed. (2007) — Ch. 20, definition of employee, printed p. 226: degree of control and differences between employees and contractors (*textbook*)
- [Canada Revenue Agency, Contract formed outside Quebec](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/employee-self-employed/contract-outside-quebec.html) — Two-step analysis of party intent and actual working relationship (*current-government-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — IV.5 — Business, Employment, and Labour Law (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-047

**Blueprint:** IV.9 · **Difficulty:** easy (provisional) · **Cognition:** understanding

A contractor finishes a bonded project but fails to pay a qualifying materials supplier that contracted directly with the bonded contractor. Which bond is designed primarily to protect eligible claimants against this non-payment?

A. A performance bond, because it automatically pays all suppliers whenever a contractor completes the work.
B. A labour and material payment bond, subject to its terms and the claimant's eligibility.
C. A bid bond, because it guarantees payment of suppliers long after the bid is accepted.
D. A contractor's professional errors-and-omissions policy, because it insures routine unpaid invoices.

**Answer:** B

**Why:**

- **A:** Performance bonds principally secure performance of the bonded contractor's obligations to the obligee; payment to eligible suppliers is a distinct bond purpose.
- **B — correct:** A labour and material payment bond is intended to address specified payment obligations to eligible labour/material claimants.
- **C:** A bid bond principally addresses bidding commitments, not ordinary non-payment of suppliers after completed performance.
- **D:** Professional liability insurance ordinarily addresses covered professional negligence risks, not a contractor's routine accounts payable.

**Learning objective:** Identify the role of a labour and material payment bond as distinct from a performance bond.

**Key point:** Distinguish bond purposes: performance protects contractual performance; labour/material payment secures eligible payment obligations.

**Common trap:** Assuming any surety or insurance policy covers every kind of contractual default.

**Jurisdiction:** Canada; a standard labour and material payment bond is assumed to cover eligible direct subcontractors/suppliers; actual wording and claimant status govern individual claims.

**References:**

- Samuels and Sanders, Practical Law of Architecture, Engineering, and Geoscience, Canadian ed. (2007) — Ch. 17, section 17.6, printed pp. 196–197: payment bonds and subcontractors (*textbook*)
- [Surety Association of Canada, Labour & Material Bonds](https://suretycanada.com/SAC/Surety-Bonds/Labour-Material-Bonds.aspx) — Nature of payment bond; direct claimants and policy conditions (*current-industry-guidance*)
- [Surety Association of Canada, Performance Bonds](https://suretycanada.com/SAC/SAC/Surety-Bonds/Performance-Bonds.aspx) — Secures performance obligations to obligee rather than every supplier payment (*current-industry-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — IV.9 — Bonds and Construction Liens (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-048

**Blueprint:** IV.11 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

At a Saskatchewan processing facility, an unplanned chemical discharge may reach a nearby drainage channel. The crew isolates the leak and begins containment. A supervisor proposes waiting for laboratory results before deciding whether anyone must notify the province. What should the responsible team do?

A. Wait for laboratory confirmation because reporting is required only after environmental damage has been measured.
B. Notify only the company's insurer because internal containment replaces provincial reporting obligations.
C. Notify the provincial spill authority promptly while continuing containment and recording facts.
D. Submit a written report in 30 days and treat that as a substitute for the initial notification.

**Answer:** C

**Why:**

- **A:** Under Saskatchewan guidance, a discharge that may harm the environment or pose safety risks requires immediate notification; certainty of damage is not the trigger described.
- **B:** An insurance notification does not replace environmental reporting duties owed to the authority with jurisdiction.
- **C — correct:** Immediate provincial notification, containment and incident documentation meet the relevant first-step obligations; any later written report is additional where required.
- **D:** A later written-report deadline for qualifying discharges does not remove the separate immediate reporting duty.

**Learning objective:** Recognize an immediate provincial environmental spill-reporting obligation distinct from a later written-report deadline.

**Key point:** Understand immediate spill notification separately from later written reporting, and identify the applicable jurisdiction.

**Common trap:** Waiting for laboratory certainty or confusing a later written report with an initial legal notification.

**Jurisdiction:** Saskatchewan; the released hazardous substance may harm the environment or pose a public health or safety risk as stated.

**References:**

- [Government of Saskatchewan, Hazardous Discharge (Spills) Reporting](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/environmental-protection-and-sustainability/hazardous-materials-and-safe-waste-management/report-hazardous-spills) — Immediate reporting of releases that may harm environment or risk public health and safety; later written report if reportable limit exceeded (*current-provincial-government*)
- Andrews, Shaw and McPhee, Canadian Professional Engineering and Geoscience: Practice and Ethics, 6th ed. (2019) — Ch. 13, environmental ethics, duties and reporting, printed pp. 305–317; historical conceptual support, not current Saskatchewan law (*textbook*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — IV.11 — Environmental Law (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-049

**Blueprint:** IV.12 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

A Saskatchewan maintenance worker is told to enter a vessel without atmospheric testing even though hazardous vapour exposure is reasonably suspected. The worker tells the supervisor that the task is unusually dangerous and refuses it. The supervisor says the worker must either enter immediately or leave the site. What is the appropriate response?

A. Follow Saskatchewan's work-refusal process, investigate and escalate unresolved concerns as prescribed.
B. Require the worker to enter first; the refusal procedure can begin only after illness or injury is recorded.
C. Automatically dismiss the worker for refusal because an employer's production schedule overrides the statutory right.
D. Ask a replacement worker to enter while the concern is assessed, because the refusal binds only the first worker.

**Answer:** A

**Why:**

- **A — correct:** The worker has raised a specified serious hazard. The supervisor must address the concern under the Saskatchewan procedure rather than ignore it or bypass escalation.
- **B:** The right concerns reasonably believed unusually dangerous work, not only hazards proven by actual injury.
- **C:** An unresolved good-faith refusal is not automatically overridden by production urgency; the prescribed process must be followed.
- **D:** Assigning another person to the same potentially dangerous task without resolving the hazard does not properly address the refusal or safety responsibilities.

**Learning objective:** Apply Saskatchewan's work-refusal procedure to a clearly identified unusually dangerous task.

**Key point:** Recognize a worker's right to refuse unusually dangerous work and the employer's duty to investigate through prescribed stages.

**Common trap:** Treating a serious work refusal as simple insubordination or shifting risk to another worker.

**Jurisdiction:** Saskatchewan; a worker has reasonable grounds to believe the specified task is unusually dangerous within The Saskatchewan Employment Act.

**References:**

- [Government of Saskatchewan, Duties and Rights of Workers](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/safety-in-the-workplace/rights-and-responsibilities-in-the-workplace/duties-and-rights-of-workers) — Work refusal under The Saskatchewan Employment Act, section 3-31; supervisor, OHC and occupational health officer stages (*current-provincial-government*)
- [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety, Right to Refuse](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/legisl/legislation/right_to_refuse.html) — Canada-wide overview with jurisdictional differences (*current-public-safety-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — IV.12 — Workers Compensation and Occupational Health & Safety (*official-blueprint*)

## Question NPPE-050

**Blueprint:** VI.2 · **Difficulty:** medium (provisional) · **Cognition:** application

An individual registrant in British Columbia is notified that the regulator will conduct a focused technical practice review after a prior assessment identified possible shortcomings in a specific method. The registrant assumes the review itself proves misconduct and automatically suspends the licence. Which explanation is best?

A. The notification is a final discipline order; the registrant should stop practising immediately without reading its terms.
B. A practice review is simply a voluntary peer workshop and the registrant has no obligation to respond.
C. A focused review addresses identified practice risks; its notice alone is not a final discipline finding or suspension.
D. The reviewer must publish a finding of misconduct before asking for any technical records from the registrant.

**Answer:** C

**Why:**

- **A:** Notification of a review is not itself a final discipline decision; actual terms of any regulatory order should be read separately.
- **B:** The described process is a regulator-administered review, not merely an optional professional-development meeting.
- **C — correct:** EGBC distinguishes reactive, targeted practice reviews from proactive individual audits and formal disciplinary findings. Consequences depend on the process and findings.
- **D:** The regulator can undertake focused examination and request information without first establishing misconduct as a concluded fact.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish an individual targeted practice review in BC from an automatic disciplinary finding or routine audit.

**Key point:** Distinguish review, audit, investigation and final discipline; procedural initiation does not predetermine findings.

**Common trap:** Assuming a targeted professional practice review automatically establishes wrongdoing or suspends licensure.

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia; Engineers and Geoscientists BC Individual Practice Review framework.

**References:**

- [Engineers and Geoscientists BC, Individual Practice Review](https://www.egbc.ca/registrants/practice-resources/individual-practice-review) — Reactive technical reviews under the Professional Governance Act, distinct from proactive audits (*current-regulator-guidance*)
- [Engineers and Geoscientists BC, Individual Audit Program](https://www.egbc.ca/Registrants/Practice-Resources/Individual-Audit-Program) — Audit is proactive and practice review reactive/targeted (*current-regulator-guidance*)
- [NPPE 2017 blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/) — VI.2 — Practice Review of Individuals (*official-blueprint*)
