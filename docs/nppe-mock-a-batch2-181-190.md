## Content and source review

Each item and all four rationales were reviewed for one best answer under its stated assumptions. The scenario details and arithmetic are author-created. The source review distinguishes binding law, regulator procedures, advisory ethics and descriptive technical guidance.

- **181:** Engineers Canada's [ethical guideline](https://engineerscanada.ca/guidelines-and-papers/public-guideline-on-the-code-of-ethics), principles 7/12, supports fair and dignified treatment. It is advisory; the question does not decide a statutory harassment offence or prescribe a universal external-reporting requirement.
- **182:** [PEO Professional Engineering Practice](https://www.peo.on.ca/sites/default/files/2020-12/PEPGuideline_Nov2020.pdf), §§10.2–10.3, printed pp. 11–12, and advisory ethics principles 2/4/5 support accurate scope and handover communication. Corrected a draft locator from section 7 to 10.2–10.3. Legal termination, notice and transfer rights are expressly settled; no universal right to terminate for non-payment or unqualified right to transfer proprietary records is asserted.
- **183:** [ISO's explanation](https://www.iso.org/insights/iso-9001-explained), §§1/3/5, identifies quality management systems and independent certification bodies. The official site now links ISO 9001:2026 while parts of the explanatory guide still mention 2015. The item tests the edition-neutral distinction between system certification and product evidence; it does not claim 2015 is the latest edition or invent transition deadlines.
- **184:** Advisory ethics principles 4/11 and [Statistics Canada's environmental accounts](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/221219/dq221219d-eng.htm) distinguish total water use and intensity. The historical statistics are conceptual background, not purported 2026 measurements. Fictional arithmetic: 100 × 10 = 1,000 m³/day; 150 × 8 = 1,200 m³/day. Intensity improves 20% while total withdrawal increases 20%; no permit-compliance conclusion follows from either measure alone.
- **185:** [Southcott Estates, 2012 SCC 51](https://decisions.scc-csc.ca/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/item/12612/index.do), paras. 24–25, supports reasonable mitigation and the defendant's burden. Official indexed judgment passages were checked; both direct HTML and PDF retrieval returned access errors, so this is not represented as a fresh full-reasons reading. The reasonable, qualified and affordable substitute and provable avoided losses are expressly supplied; the question does not impose unreasonable mitigation, automatic waiver or an absolute duty to accept every substitute.
- **186:** Current official indexed [Ontario Limitations Act, 2002](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02l24), s.15(1)–(2), confirms the ordinary ultimate fifteen-year period despite another unexpired period. Direct e-Laws retrieval returned 403. PEO's practice guide §10.8 also identifies the fifteen-year rule; that older summary is corroboration, not a substitute for exceptions. The stem expressly excludes any qualification preserving the claim, including suspensions and extensions; it does not assert every claim expires fifteen years after a seal, discovery or expert report. Case-specific legal assessment remains necessary outside this hypothetical.
- **187:** [PIPEDA Schedule 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/page-7.html), clauses 4.1/4.1.3, read directly; official consolidation current to September 21, 2026. The [OPC September 10, 2026 guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/appropriate-handling-of-personal-information/gd_third-party_202609/) was also read. It is open for comments until December 4, 2026 and is not a new statute. The item is grounded in the statutory principle and stipulates PIPEDA applicability and the existing authorized processing purpose.
- **188:** PEO's current [volunteer FAQ](https://www.peo.on.ca/knowledge-centre/frequently-asked-questions/volunteers-faq) expressly includes unpaid professional services; its [Certificate of Authorization guidance](https://www.peo.on.ca/apply/certificate-authorization) identifies s.12(2) and the distinct entity authorization. Scope is Ontario, independent services directly to the public, with no applicable exception. This is not a nationwide volunteer-licensing rule.
- **189:** Read PEO's [November 7, 2025 Discipline Committee Rules](https://www.peo.on.ca/sites/default/files/2025-11/rules-of-procedure-dic-nov2025.pdf), rules 3.3(2), 3.7(4)/(7), 3.8(1)/(3)/(5). Older 2018/2023 rules also appear in searches; they were not used as the current requirements. A late expert report needs leave; neither automatic admission nor automatic case dismissal follows. The question avoids a rote deadline calculation and preserves panel discretion and opportunity to respond.
- **190:** [Statistics Canada visualization guidance](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/89-26-0005/892600052022001-eng.htm), titles and axes, read directly. Visible heights 0.3 and 0.1 above 4.5% have a 3:1 ratio, but rates 4.8% and 4.6% differ by 0.2 percentage points, about 4.2% relatively. No causal or significance conclusion is requested; no universal prohibition on nonzero line-chart axes is implied.
- All mappings checked against the [official NPPE blueprint](https://www.nppexam.ca/en/preparation/blueprint/), which still labels the 2017 blueprint current and retains a review announcement. V.2 includes business practice permits; VI.1 includes disciplinary procedures. Recheck the blueprint before release.

The ordinary textbook references and historical limitations already recorded in the Learning reviews remain background. No new page-level textbook reading is claimed for this batch, and no instructor test-bank material was used.
