## Content review and corrections

- **271:** Tests who belongs to the public served by engineering, not an invented universal accessibility-code requirement. The position statement is identified as advisory; historical statistics in it are not reproduced as current facts.
- **272:** Tests habituation and character formation rather than repeating the earlier courage/mean classification. Custom alone is not represented as ethical justification. Alternatives concern the stated rationale, not whether other theories could also endorse honesty.
- **273:** Makes the client’s independence requirement and the reviewer’s prior decision-making explicit. A fixed fee and sincere objectivity do not remove the incompatible role. Factual participation is distinguished from acting as the independent assessor.
- **274:** Stipulates an adopted code that permits alternatives subject to AHJ acceptance. Technical evidence is necessary, but prior use and warranties do not themselves establish acceptance. National model-code guidance is not treated as automatic local law.
- **275:** Distinguishes confidentiality protection from malware checks. Approval and screening are required by the hypothetical firm’s process; screening is not claimed to eliminate all risk.
- **276:** Replaced a draft pump-cost example resembling Learning 163 with conveyor maintenance shutdown consequences that management has not received. The tested step is informing operational planning, not rejecting a client’s already informed preference. Both options meet safety/legal requirements; no external danger report is invented.
- **277:** Independently read the official Supreme Court Reports decision in *Wastech*, particularly paras. 63–71 and 76–77. The fictional contract expressly gives the discretion a coordination purpose; unrelated debt pressure is not that purpose. Honest disclosure alone is insufficient, but good faith does not require maximizing the counterparty’s profit. This is Canadian common law, not a statement of Quebec’s Civil Code.
- **278:** Limits all relevant acts to outside Canada. No claim is made that overseas manufacture prevents liability for a later Canadian import/sale, or that other foreign rights cannot apply. Patent Act section 42 and CIPO’s territorial explanation were read directly.
- **279:** Uses PEO’s current FAQ, which permits sufficiently knowledgeable validators from another workplace and retains PEO’s eligibility/final-assessment role. Avoids falsely making supervisor title, same-employer status or technical responsibility an absolute requirement. Both people meet stipulated licensing requirements; only one knows the examples sufficiently well.
- **280:** Replaced a draft random-audit/guilt scenario too close to Diagnostic 030. The accepted item uses the individual audit guide’s written-extension procedure. A request receives an approval/denial decision; no automatic new deadline or predetermined sanction is asserted.

The batch was compared against the 490 existing stems/families and nearby concept examples. A token-overlap screen supported the manual comparison; it is not an originality certification.
