1 · Ask the right question

Three charts, three different questions

A process specialist is investigating wall-thickness differences between two pipe lines. Before fitting a model, they want to understand the distribution of measurements, the consistency of each operating group, and whether the shift effect changes between lines. These charts help answer those questions visually.

What each Minitab quality chart is for
ChartQuestion it answersInputsTypical use case
Symmetry plotIs a distribution balanced around its median?One numeric variable, such as thickness or downtime.Explore skewness before choosing summaries or an analysis approach.
Variability chartWhich factor combinations have different averages and different within-group spread?A numeric response and grouping factors, with repeated observations per cell.Find the line/shift/position combinations that are least consistent.
Multi-vari chartHow do factor-level means differ, and does one factor's effect depend on another?A numeric response and two or more meaningful factor levels.Look for position effects and Line × Shift interactions before ANOVA or regression.
One important distinction: these are exploratory quality tools. They do not have control limits and do not establish time-ordered process stability. A symmetry plot does not establish normality. A visible factor pattern does not establish statistical significance or causation.

Understand what one row means

In Symmetry_Data, the wall-thickness sample and downtime sample are independent datasets. Their placement on the same row does not pair a pipe with a stoppage. Obs is just a row identifier.

In Factor_Data, each row is one measurement from one distinct pipe at its assigned Leading, Middle, or Trailing location. A pipe is measured only once in this example. The data are not repeated measurements of the same pipe at three locations. This distinction matters if you later select an inferential model.

Factor, level, response, and cell: Wall_mm is the numeric response. Line, Shift, and Position are factors. A/B, Day/Night, and Leading/Middle/Trailing are their levels. A cell is one complete combination, such as B / Night / Leading. There are 2 × 2 × 3 = 12 cells, with 5 different pipes per cell, so there are 60 measurements. Every combination is represented.

3 · Distribution shape

Symmetry plot: are the two tails balanced?

A symmetry plot compares observations above and below the median. Imagine folding a distribution at its median: values at corresponding distances on the two sides should line up if the distribution is symmetric. A few long stoppages can stretch the right tail even when most stoppages are short.

Use it for: exploring wall-thickness distributions, cycle times, waiting times, repair durations, or other numeric measurements. It helps you see skewness that a single mean can hide. Examine a histogram as well to see features such as multiple peaks.

How the plot is constructed

  1. Sort the observations and find the median.
  2. Pair the closest observation below the median with the closest above it. Continue outward through the tails.
  3. Plot the upper observation's distance above the median on the horizontal axis, and the lower observation's distance below the median on the vertical axis.
  4. Compare the plotted points with the diagonal equality line.
dabove=xupper−mdbelow=m−xlower

Here, m is the sample median. The diagonal represents equal distances on the two sides.

Read the axes before judging skewness
Point patternMeaningWhy
Near the diagonalApproximately symmetric.Above-median and below-median distances are similar.
Diverges below the diagonalRight-skewed.The upper tail extends farther: horizontal distance is larger.
Diverges above the diagonalLeft-skewed.The lower tail extends farther: vertical distance is larger.

Worked example: the wall median is 9.530 mm. The extreme pair is 9.340 and 9.720 mm, so both distances are 0.190 mm and the point lies on the diagonal. The downtime median is 11.5 min. Its extreme pair is 3 and 190 min: the horizontal distance is 178.5 min and the vertical distance is 8.5 min. That point lies far below the diagonal, showing a long right tail.

4 · Consistency within groups

Variability chart: compare averages and spread

A variability chart groups individual measurements by factor levels so you can compare their means and their within-cell variation. Two groups can have the same average and very different consistency. Conversely, their averages can differ while their spreads remain similar.

Use it for: comparing machines, lines, operators, shifts, suppliers, batches, or positions. In this example, the question is: “Which Line / Shift / Position combinations produce the greatest variation in wall thickness?”

Read the parts of the chart

  • Individual points show the actual wall readings.
  • Cell mean shows the average for one complete factor combination.
  • Range bar joins that cell's observed minimum and maximum. A longer bar indicates more spread. It is not a confidence interval or a control limit.
  • Connecting lines of cell means make changes in average response easier to follow.
  • Overall mean is an optional horizontal reference line. It does not represent a specification or target.
  • Standard deviation chart is an optional second graph comparing sample SDs within the cells. Five pipes per cell make SD estimation possible here.
R=xmax−xmins=∑i=1n(xi−x¯)2n−1

Calculate the range and sample SD separately for each cell. A mean uses the five measurements in that cell.

Why include Position? Leading, Middle, and Trailing means intentionally differ. If you group only by Line and Shift, the spread of the 15 readings mixes those position differences with the scatter of pipes measured at the same position. Including Position isolates the five-pipe comparison within each location.

5 · Factor effects and interactions

Multi-vari chart: does one factor change another's effect?

A multi-vari chart displays response means across combinations of categorical factors. It lets you compare effects at several levels in one view. In this case, compare positions within shifts and shifts within lines.

Use it for: exploring machine × operator, supplier × material, line × shift, or other factor relationships before a formal analysis. Its central value here is showing that the shift effect is different on Lines A and B.

How it differs from a variability chart

The variability chart emphasizes the observations and spread within each cell. The multi-vari chart emphasizes mean patterns across factors. It can display individual observations too, but the means-first view makes the factor structure easier to follow. Choose the one that answers your investigation question.

What an interaction means

An interaction occurs when the effect of one factor depends on the level of another. Here, changing Day to Night increases average wall thickness by 0.010 mm on Line A and 0.160 mm on Line B. The unequal changes suggest a Line × Shift interaction. Lines do not have to cross for an interaction to exist.

ΔA=9.540−9.530=0.010 mmΔB=9.720−9.560=0.160 mmΔB−ΔA=0.150 mm

Position effect: Trailing-position means exceed Leading-position means by 0.080 mm in all four Line / Shift groups. That position effect is consistent across those groups in this deliberately constructed example.

Step by step: create a multi-vari chart in Minitab

  1. Activate Factor_Data. Use the original 60-row worksheet.
  2. Open the command. Choose Stat → Quality Tools → Multi-Vari Chart.
  3. Assign the response. In Response, enter Wall_mm.
  4. Assign Factor 1. Select Position. This is the innermost comparison.
  5. Assign the remaining factors. Set Factor 2 to Shift and Factor 3 to Line. Leave Factor 4 blank. This order is the reverse of the outermost-first entry order used for the variability chart.
  6. Choose the display. Click Options. Select Connect means for Factor 1, Connect means for Factor 2, and Connect means for Factor 3. For your first graph, leave Display individual data points unchecked so you can concentrate on the means.
  7. Create and read the graph. Click OK in Options and then OK in the main dialog. Read the legend, compare positions within each shift, then compare shifts within each line.
  8. Try the alternative view. Reopen the command with the same factor order and select Display individual data points. Compare how the added observations show scatter around the means. Changing factor order changes the viewing emphasis, not the measurements.

Data requirements: Minitab supports up to four factors for Multi-Vari Chart. Each factor needs at least two levels, and observations must populate at least 60% of all factor-level combinations. Our 12 of 12 cells provide 100% coverage. Good replication and a suitable sampling design are still needed for meaningful inference.

Review Minitab output

Read Position within Shift within Line

  1. Start with the legend. In this output, the blue Position markers represent cell means, red squares represent Shift means within each line, and green diamonds represent Line means. Use the labels as well as colors.
  2. Compare the blue Position means. Leading to Middle to Trailing increases by 0.040 mm at each step, or 0.080 mm from Leading to Trailing.
  3. Compare Day and Night within A. The shift means are 9.530 and 9.540 mm, a 0.010 mm increase.
  4. Compare Day and Night within B. The shift means are 9.560 and 9.720 mm, a 0.160 mm increase.
  5. Compare Line means carefully. A averages 9.535 mm and B averages 9.640 mm. Averaging all shifts together conceals the much larger Night effect on B.
  6. Report the interaction. “The effect of Shift depends on Line: the Night increase is much larger on B. Analyze the Line × Shift combination rather than treating Night as a uniform effect.” A suitable ANOVA or regression model can test and estimate the effect after design and assumption checks.

6 · Practice, check, explain

Complete the investigation

Use your Minitab graphs and the original dataset. Write your own interpretation before revealing each answer.

Quick calculation check

Calculate the B / Night / Leading range from its five measurements: 9.600, 9.640, 9.680, 9.720, and 9.760 mm.

Enter your result, then select Check my calculation.

  1. Explain the two symmetry plots. Which sample is symmetric and which has a long right tail?
    Check the explanation
    Wall_mm is symmetric around 9.530 mm because matching tail distances lie on the diagonal. Downtime_min is right-skewed: above-median distances exceed below-median distances, moving the points below the diagonal.
  2. Separate the mean problem from the consistency problem. Why should B / Night receive attention?
    Check the explanation
    Its overall Line / Shift mean is 9.720 mm, and each position cell has a range of 0.160 mm and sample SD about 0.06325 mm. Both the average and the within-position spread stand out. Specifications are not included, so this alone does not show whether the product fails a specification.
  3. Explain the interaction. Why would a single Day-to-Night average change be incomplete?
    Check the explanation
    The change is only 0.010 mm on A and 0.160 mm on B. The equally weighted pooled shift change is 0.085 mm, which hides these two different responses. Keep Line × Shift in the investigation.
  4. Investigate the sampling structure. How would the analysis change if Leading, Middle, and Trailing readings came from the same pipe?
    Check the explanation
    Measurements on the same pipe would be related. Record a shared Pipe_ID and use an appropriate repeated-measures or mixed-effects design when doing inference. Do not count those related readings as independent pipes. The graphs can still be useful descriptively.
  5. Write a short recommendation. Name the group, quote mean and spread evidence, identify checks, and state the next analysis.
    Read a worked recommendation
    “Prioritize Line B during Night. Its mean wall thickness is 9.720 mm and its within-position range is 0.160 mm, compared with 9.560 mm and 0.080 mm on B during Day. Check measurement-system performance, setup records, and operating conditions. Collect independent replicated data and use a model that includes Line × Shift, with assumption checks, to assess the pattern.”

Excel Use Cases

Enter these formulas in unused cells in Excel. Do not overwrite the raw data columns. All results below refer to the original practice dataset.

  1. Verify the symmetry center. =MEDIAN(Symmetry_Data!B2:B31) gives 9.530 mm. =MEDIAN(Symmetry_Data!C2:C31) gives 11.5 min. =COUNT(Symmetry_Data!B2:B31) gives 30.
  2. Build the extreme wall pair. =INDEX(SORT(Symmetry_Data!B2:B31),1) gives 9.340; use row number 30 for 9.720. Subtract the median from the upper value for horizontal distance, and subtract the lower value from the median for vertical distance. Both distances equal 0.190 mm.
  3. Verify B / Night / Leading's mean. =AVERAGEIFS(Factor_Data!E2:E61,Factor_Data!B2:B61,"B",Factor_Data!C2:C61,"Night",Factor_Data!D2:D61,"Leading") gives 9.680 mm. Drop the Position criterion to obtain the B / Night mean, 9.720 mm.
  4. Verify the cell's replication. =COUNTIFS(Factor_Data!B2:B61,"B",Factor_Data!C2:C61,"Night",Factor_Data!D2:D61,"Leading") gives 5.
  5. Verify its range. =MAX(Factor_Data!E47:E51)-MIN(Factor_Data!E47:E51) gives 0.160 mm. Those rows are the five B / Night / Leading pipes in the original sheet. If you sort or reorder the data, select the cell with criteria instead: use MAXIFS and MINIFS with Line = B, Shift = Night, and Position = Leading.
  6. Verify its sample SD. =STDEV.S(Factor_Data!E47:E51) gives approximately 0.06324555 mm. A sort-safe Microsoft 365 formula is =STDEV.S(FILTER(Factor_Data!E2:E61,(Factor_Data!B2:B61="B")*(Factor_Data!C2:C61="Night")*(Factor_Data!D2:D61="Leading"))). Multiplication means all three conditions must be true.
  7. Verify the interaction. Use AVERAGEIFS to obtain each Line / Shift mean. Calculate Night minus Day within each line, then subtract A's change from B's. The difference is 0.150 mm. The overall mean =AVERAGE(Factor_Data!E2:E61) is 9.5875 mm.

Version alternative: if FILTER or SORT is unavailable, copy the selected measurements into an unused range and sort/filter them with Excel's data commands before calculating. For selected-cell SD, use the five actual measurements; do not use SUBTOTAL or a whole-range STDEV.S that still includes other groups.

Exam Tips

For ASQ CSSBB/CQE study and workplace reasoning, focus on selecting the tool and interpreting the evidence rather than memorizing screen appearance. These are study reminders, not claims about specific exam questions.

  • Symmetry and normality differ. A symmetric two-cluster distribution is still not bell-shaped.
  • Read the axis orientation. With distance above on X and distance below on Y, below the diagonal means right skew.
  • Compare like cells. Spread across positions mixes a systematic position effect with within-position scatter.
  • Know the denominator. STDEV.S and the displayed sample SD use n − 1, not n.
  • An interaction is a changing effect. Unequal Day-to-Night changes matter even if plotted lines do not cross.
  • Respect the design. Repeated readings on one pipe are not independent pipes. Consider replication, measurement-system quality, and randomization before inference.
  • Choose the next tool deliberately. Use supported ANOVA/regression for inferential effects and an appropriate time-ordered control chart for process stability.

9 · Apply it at work

Take the same approach to your own process

Start with a clear question and a sampling plan. Record meaningful factor levels and a unique observation or part ID. Explore the response distribution, compare means and within-group spread, then inspect interactions. Use the findings to plan the next measurement or model.

Your deliverable: save the two symmetry graphs, the variability chart plus optional SD graph, and the multi-vari chart in your Minitab project. Write a short recommendation that names the group, quotes the mean and spread, and explains the interaction without claiming a cause.
