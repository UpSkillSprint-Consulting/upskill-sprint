      Map stop 02 · Model chance

      Probability distributions: tails, densities and critical values

      Goal: turn a value into a probability, or a probability into a value.

      One naming rule covers all of them. .DIST gives a probability (set cumulative to TRUE for the CDF, FALSE for the density), .INV turns a probability back into a value, .RT means right tail and .2T means both tails. With no suffix, the function works on the left tail.

      Tail translator: pick the tail your test uses

      Example: \(t = 2.10\) with \(9\) degrees of freedom.

      Right tail: =T.DIST.RT(2.10, 9) = 0.0326

      Same tail in other families: CHISQ.DIST.RT, F.DIST.RT; for the normal use 1-NORM.S.DIST(z, TRUE).

      Map stop 03 · Infer

      Statistical inference: confidence intervals in Excel

      Goal: give a range that probably holds the true value.

      Excel has only two dedicated interval functions, and both return the margin of error, not the interval. Every other interval is a standard error times a critical value from an .INV function.

      \[ \bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2,\,n-1}\,\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} \]

      where \(\bar{x}\) is the sample mean, \(s\) the sample standard deviation, \(n\) the sample size and \(t_{\alpha/2,\,n-1}\) the two-sided critical value. CONFIDENCE.T returns the whole \(\pm\) part in one step.

      The two interval functions
      FunctionSyntaxReturnsWhen to use it
      CONFIDENCE.TCONFIDENCE.T(alpha, standard_dev, size)Margin of error using t (half-width)Confidence interval for a mean, sigma unknown
      CONFIDENCE.NORMCONFIDENCE.NORM(alpha, standard_dev, size)Margin of error using z (half-width)Confidence interval for a mean, sigma known

      Interval recipes

      Building every common interval
      IntervalExcel
      Mean, sigma unknown=AVERAGE(r) ± CONFIDENCE.T(α, STDEV.S(r), COUNT(r))
      Mean, sigma known=AVERAGE(r) ± CONFIDENCE.NORM(α, σ, n)
      Variance=(n-1)*s^2/CHISQ.INV.RT(α/2, n-1) to =(n-1)*s^2/CHISQ.INV(α/2, n-1)
      Proportion (Wald)=p ± NORM.S.INV(1-α/2)*SQRT(p*(1-p)/n)
      Proportion (exact)=BETA.INV(α/2, x, n-x+1) to =BETA.INV(1-α/2, x+1, n-x)
      Poisson rate (exact)=CHISQ.INV(α/2, 2*c)/2 to =CHISQ.INV.RT(α/2, 2*(c+1))/2
      Correlation=FISHERINV(FISHER(r) ± NORM.S.INV(1-α/2)/SQRT(n-3))
      Regression slope=SLOPE(y,x) ± T.INV.2T(α, n-2)*INDEX(LINEST(y,x,TRUE,TRUE),2,1)
      Sample size for a mean=ROUNDUP((NORM.S.INV(1-α/2)*σ/E)^2, 0)

      Write ± as two cells. Excel has no ± operator: put the lower limit and the upper limit in their own cells.

      Map stop 04 · Decide

      Hypothesis testing in Excel: the four test functions and the rest

      Goal: decide whether a difference is real or noise.

      Excel's four test functions return only a p-value: no test statistic, no degrees of freedom. For any other test, compute the statistic, then turn it into a p-value with a .DIST function. Reject \(H_0\) when \(p \le \alpha\).

      Test picker: what is your question?

      Pick a question to see the Excel formula, the Minitab path and where it lives on the map.

      The four test functions
      FunctionSyntaxReturnsWhen to use it
      T.TESTT.TEST(array1, array2, tails, type)p-value; type 1 paired, 2 pooled, 3 WelchComparing two means
      Z.TESTZ.TEST(array, x, [sigma])Upper-tail p-value of a one-sample z testMean against a target when sigma is known
      F.TESTF.TEST(array1, array2)Two-tailed p-value for equal variancesChecking variances before choosing T.TEST type
      CHISQ.TESTCHISQ.TEST(actual_range, expected_range)p-value for independence or fitCounts by category; contingency tables

      Build any other test from a statistic

      \[ t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}} \]

      where \(\mu_0\) is the target. The same pattern, statistic then .DIST, covers the rest.

      Statistic, two-sided p-value and critical value
      TestTest statisticTwo-sided p-valueCritical value
      1-sample z=(AVERAGE(r)-μ0)/(σ/SQRT(n))=2*(1-NORM.S.DIST(ABS(z),TRUE))=NORM.S.INV(1-α/2)
      1-sample t=(AVERAGE(r)-μ0)/(STDEV.S(r)/SQRT(n))=T.DIST.2T(ABS(t), n-1)=T.INV.2T(α, n-1)
      Paired tt on the differences=T.TEST(a, b, 2, 1)=T.INV.2T(α, n-1)
      2-sample t, pooledpooled t=T.TEST(a, b, 2, 2)=T.INV.2T(α, n1+n2-2)
      2-sample t, WelchWelch t=T.TEST(a, b, 2, 3)Welch df by hand
      1 variance=(n-1)*VAR.S(r)/σ0^2=2*MIN(CHISQ.DIST(x,n-1,TRUE), CHISQ.DIST.RT(x,n-1))CHISQ.INV and CHISQ.INV.RT at α/2
      2 variances=VAR.S(a)/VAR.S(b)=F.TEST(a, b)F.INV and F.INV.RT at α/2
      1 proportion, exactdefectives x=2*MIN(BINOM.DIST(x,n,p0,TRUE), 1-BINOM.DIST(x-1,n,p0,TRUE))BINOM.INV(n, p0, α/2)
      2 proportions=(p1-p2)/SQRT(p*(1-p)*(1/n1+1/n2))=2*(1-NORM.S.DIST(ABS(z),TRUE))=NORM.S.INV(1-α/2)
      Correlation is zero=r*SQRT(n-2)/SQRT(1-r^2)=T.DIST.2T(ABS(t), n-2)=T.INV.2T(α, n-2)
      One-way ANOVA=MS_between/MS_within=F.DIST.RT(F, k-1, N-k)=F.INV.RT(α, k-1, N-k)
      Chi-square, any df=SUM((O-E)^2/E)=CHISQ.DIST.RT(x, df)=CHISQ.INV.RT(α, df)
      • Z.TEST is upper-tailTwo-sided p is =2*MIN(Z.TEST(r,μ0,σ), 1-Z.TEST(r,μ0,σ)).
      • One-tailed T.TEST ignores directionCheck which mean is larger before you trust it.
      • CHISQ.TEST needs expected countsEach expected count is its row total times its column total, divided by the grand total.

      Correlation: Pearson r, its p-value and its interval

      Goal: check whether two measures move together in a straight line.

      CORREL and PEARSON return the same \(r\). Excel has no p-value or Spearman function, so test \(r\) with a \(t\) statistic and get Spearman by correlating ranks.

      Excel use cases: seven worked examples

      Made-up plant data. Every value was cross-checked in SciPy, and Excel returns the same numbers.

      1. 1-sample t and interval. Ten tensile coupons (MPa): 492, 488, 495, 481, 499, 490, 486, 493, 497, 489. Is the mean above 485? =AVERAGE(A2:A11) gives 491.0 and =STDEV.S(A2:A11) gives 5.375.
        \[ t = \frac{491.0 - 485}{5.375/\sqrt{10}} = 3.530 \]
        One-sided p: =T.DIST.RT(3.530, 9) = 0.0032. Interval: =CONFIDENCE.T(0.05, 5.375, 10) = 3.845, so 487.2 to 494.8 MPa. The mean is above 485.
      2. Paired t. Crane cycle times (s) before 62, 58, 71, 66, 54, 69, 60, 63 and after 48, 47, 55, 50, 46, 52, 49, 51. =T.TEST(before, after, 2, 1) = 0.000007. The mean saving is 13.1 s.
      3. Variance check, then the right t-test. Line A has 8 values and line B has 10. =F.TEST(A, B) = 0.012, so the variances differ. =T.TEST(A, B, 2, 3) = 0.317: no evidence the means differ.
      4. Chi-square test of independence. Defect type by shift: observed 18, 7, 5 and 12, 15, 13. Build expected counts, then =CHISQ.TEST(observed, expected) = 0.041 with 2 df. Defect type depends on shift.
      5. Exact proportion test. 7 defectives in 200 pipes against a 2% target. =1-BINOM.DIST(6, 200, 0.02, TRUE) = 0.109. The expected count is 4, below 5, so report the exact value, not the z approximation (0.065).
      6. Poisson probability. 1.2 surface defects per pipe on average. The chance of 3 or more is =1-POISSON.DIST(2, 1.2, TRUE) = 0.121.
      7. Solver blend. Shred at 420 per tonne and 0.22% Cu, HMS at 380 and 0.30%, pig iron at 520 and 0.01%. Minimize =SUMPRODUCT(cost, share) with Cu at most 0.18% and pig iron at most 25%. Simplex LP gives 59.4% shred, 15.6% HMS and 25% pig iron, at 438.75 per tonne.

      Exam tips

      • The exam tests reading, not keystrokes. CSSBB and CQE are open-book and calculator-based. Expect Excel or Minitab output to interpret, not software to run.
      • Tables are .INV functions. A two-tailed t table value is T.INV.2T, a chi-square table is CHISQ.INV.RT and an F table is F.INV.RT. Use them to check your lookups.
      • Know the ToolPak labels. "P(T<=t) two-tail" is the two-sided p-value; "t Critical two-tail" is the two-sided critical value.
      • Sample data means STDEV.S. A population formula on sample data is a classic distractor.
      • Legacy names in prep books. TINV is two-tailed; modern T.INV is left-tailed.
      • Real projects. Check normality and equal variances before trusting t and ANOVA, and confirm an optimized setting with runs before changing standard work.
