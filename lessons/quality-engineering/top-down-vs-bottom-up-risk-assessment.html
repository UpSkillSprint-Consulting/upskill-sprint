Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Risk Assessment

Every risk tool digs in one of two directions. Top-down vs. bottom-up risk assessment comes down to one question: do you start from the bad outcome and dig for causes, or start from each part and ask what it could break?

↓Deductive · outcome → causesTop-down

“This bad thing happened, or could. What causes it?”

Think: the detective
↕Event → both waysMiddle-out

Start at the event. Dig back to causes, walk forward to effects.

Bow-tie · HAZOP
↑Inductive · cause → effectsBottom-up

“If this part fails, what does it break?”

Think: the inspector

Poster: top-down digs from the outcome down to causes, like a detective. Middle-out starts at the event and works both ways. Bottom-up walks from each part up to its effects, like an inspector.

Part 1 · Two directions

Climb the system ladder both ways

Every product or process stacks up like a ladder: parts make steps, steps make a line, the line makes what the customer gets. Pick a direction, then press Next step and watch which rung you visit first.

Part 2 · The toolbox

Meet the 9 tools, sorted by direction

Colour tells you the direction. The chips tell you the timing: proactive (before a failure) or reactive (after one). Open any card for a steel-mill example.

Top-down

Outcome → causes · deductive

  • Fault tree analysis (FTA)

    One top event, traced down through AND / OR gates to basic causes.

    • Proactive
    • Reactive

    “Pipe ruptures in service” as the top event, traced to wall loss, low toughness, or a seam defect that UT missed.

  • RCA: 5-Why · Fishbone

    Start from the defect you saw. Ask why, or brainstorm the 6M bones, until you hit the root cause.

    • Reactive

    “Why did this heat fail its Charpy test?” Why? Grain too coarse. Why? Finish rolling ran hot. Why? …

Middle-out

Event → both ways

  • Bow-tie

    The event sits in the knot. Causes and prevention barriers on the left, consequences and recovery barriers on the right.

    • Proactive

    Show the board every barrier between “pipe ruptures” and a spill: UT, hydrotest and Charpy on the left; leak detection and shutdown on the right.

  • HAZOP

    Guide word + parameter = deviation (“no flow”). Work back to causes and forward to consequences, node by node.

    • Proactive

    Review the caster’s mould cooling-water loop on its P&ID before start-up. Try it yourself in Part 5.

Bottom-up

Cause → effects · inductive

  • FMEA / FMECA

    Every failure mode of every step, rated for severity, occurrence and detection.

    • Proactive

    A PFMEA row for ERW seam welding: “cold weld” → leak in hydrotest or service.

  • HACCP

    Step by step: list each hazard, then set critical control points and limits.

    • Proactive

    Built for food and drugs. In a mill, think of a food-grade coating line.

  • PHA

    Preliminary Hazard Analysis: an early scan of hazards for each part of a new system.

    • Proactive

    A concept for a new pickling line, before the detailed design exists.

  • HAZID

    Hazard identification: a broad first scan of a layout or concept, including how systems affect each other.

    • Proactive

    The site layout for a new acid regeneration plant: what if acid storage sits next to the crane bay?

  • What-If / SWIFT

    Structured “What if…?” prompts at each step. Faster and lighter than FMEA.

    • Proactive

    “What if the crane drops a bundle at the loading bay?” Walk every step that way.

Why HAZOP is “middle-out”: textbooks file it as top-down and as bottom-up. It really starts at a deviation in the middle and works both ways, just like the bow-tie.

Part 3 · Timing

Proactive or reactive? Before or after the failure

A proactive tool finds risk before anything breaks. A reactive tool explains a failure that already happened, so you can fix it and stop it coming back.

Proactive

Before anything breaks

  • FMEA
  • HACCP
  • PHA
  • HAZID
  • SWIFT
  • HAZOP
  • Bow-tie
  • FTA at design
  • HAZAN
!
FAILURE

Reactive

After it breaks

  • 5-Why
  • Fishbone
  • FTA in an investigation
  • CAPA / 8D
Direction
≠ timing

Exam trap: top-down does not mean reactive. FTA is top-down, yet engineers often build it at the design stage, long before anything fails. Ask two separate questions: which way? and before or after?

Part 5 · HAZOP

Spin the HAZOP guide word machine

HAZOP uses seven guide words (guide, not “guild”). Each one, paired with a process parameter, names a deviation from design intent. The team then asks four questions about it.

Guide word+Parameter=Deviation

Node: caster mould cooling water. Pick a guide word and a parameter.

Guide word

Parameter

A made-up example for teaching. Your plant’s HAZOP team will find its own causes and safeguards.

The N/A cards are the point. Many combinations mean nothing. The team still says them out loud and rules them out. That discipline is what makes HAZOP more complete than a free brainstorm.

HAZOP fits continuous processes with flow, pressure, temperature, level or composition: caster cooling water, EAF off-gas, ladle argon stirring. For final pipe inspection, use a PFMEA.

Where HAZOP sits: HAZID → HAZOP → HAZAN

HAZOP is the middle of three studies. Trevor Kletz put it simply: HAZOP identifies hazards, HAZAN assesses them. HAZID comes first, while the plant is still a layout. Tap each stage.

HAZID: hazard identification

  • AsksWhat hazards exist here at all?
  • Looks atThe layout or concept, and how systems affect each other
  • You finish withA hazard register
  • Direction · timingBottom-up · proactive
  • ISO 310001 · Identify
  • Pipe-mill exampleAcid storage next to the crane bay on a new layout

Pipe-mill examples are made up for teaching.

Big hazard staring at you? Kletz notes that an obvious major hazard can go to HAZAN before the HAZOP is done. Don’t wait to size it.

Part 6 · Use both

Fault tree lab: why FMEA needs a partner

FMEA looks at one failure mode at a time. A fault tree’s AND gate catches what it misses: two things going wrong together. Move the sliders to see how each cause drives the top event.

0.055%About 5.5 pipes in 10,000 rupture. The seam path is the biggest.

The gate maths, for independent events:

\[ P(\text{S}) = P(\text{D}) \times P(\text{M}) \] \[ P(\text{top}) = 1 - \big(1 - P(\text{W})\big)\big(1 - P(\text{T})\big)\big(1 - P(\text{S})\big) \]

where \(P(\text{W})\), \(P(\text{T})\), \(P(\text{D})\) and \(P(\text{M})\) are the chances of each basic event per pipe, and \(P(\text{S})\) is the chance that a seam defect escapes.

Run the PFMEA across the whole rolling and finishing line for coverage. Then build a fault tree for each catastrophic event, so combinations get caught too.

Putting numbers on one hazard like this is HAZAN (hazard analysis). The answer tells you how far to go in reducing the risk.

Part 7 · The big picture

Where the tools plug into ISO 31000

ISO 31000:2018 is the international guideline for managing risk. Its process (clause 6) calls risk identification, analysis and evaluation together risk assessment. That is where every tool on this page lives.

Risk assessment = steps 1 to 3

  1. Scope

    Context and risk criteria: how much risk is OK?

  2. 1 · Identify

    What could go wrong?

    • HAZID
    • HAZOP
    • SWIFT
    • PHA
    • FMEA modes

  3. 2 · Analyze

    How, how likely, how bad?

    • HAZAN
    • FTA
    • Bow-tie
    • FMEA S·O·D

  4. 3 · Evaluate

    Is it acceptable?

    • HAZAN
    • Risk matrix
    • RPN / AP

  5. 4 · Treat

    Act on the risk

    • FMEA actions
    • Barriers
    • HACCP CCPs

↺ Monitoring & reviewRCA and CAPA after failures feed back into the assessment

Communication & consultation runs through every step: a HAZOP or FMEA is a team sport.

Recording & reporting: FMEA worksheets, HAZOP logs, the risk register.

Proactive tools feed steps 1 to 4. Reactive tools feed monitoring and review, which updates the assessment. ISO 31000’s companion, IEC 31010:2019, describes each technique in detail. ISO 31000:2018 is still current; a revision is in progress.

Part 8 · Watch out

Five mistakes to avoid

Don’t be that team

  1. “Top-down = reactive”Direction is not timing. A design-stage FTA is proactive.
  2. Trusting FMEA aloneIt rates one failure mode at a time. Add FTA for combinations.
  3. HAZOP on an inspection benchHAZOP is for continuous flows. Use PFMEA for discrete steps.
  4. Skipping the N/A guide wordsRuling them out on record is what makes HAZOP complete.
  5. Stopping at “operator error”Keep asking why until you reach a cause you can fix.
Do it in software

Statistics Implementation

Psst. Excel can run your fault-tree gates and FMEA ratings in one line each, and the exam loves asking how.You found the secret level. Gate maths, RPN tricks and menu paths are all below. Unlock the cheat codesHide the cheat codes

Excel functions

FunctionSyntaxPurposeWhen to use it
PRODUCT=PRODUCT(B2:B3)Multiplies the cellsAND gate: all inputs must happen (independent events)
1-PRODUCT(1-…)=1-PRODUCT(1-B2:B4)Chance that at least one input happensOR gate. Works directly in Excel 365; older Excel needs Ctrl+Shift+Enter
PRODUCT (RPN)=PRODUCT(D2:F2)Severity × Occurrence × DetectionClassic FMEA risk priority number
RANK.EQ=RANK.EQ(G2,$G$2:$G$40)Rank of one RPN in the listFind the top risks to act on first
SORTBY=SORTBY(A2:G40,G2:G40,-1)Sorts rows by RPN, highest firstA live “worst first” view (Excel 365)
COUNTIFS=COUNTIFS(D2:D40,">=9")Counts rows meeting a ruleHow many failure modes have severity 9 or 10?

Excel use cases

  • Fault tree: put W, T, D and M in B2:B5 as decimals. =B4*B5 gives S. =1-PRODUCT(1-B2:B3,1-B6) with S in B6 gives the top event. Try 0.0002, 0.0001, 0.005 and 0.05: the top event comes out near 0.00055, matching the lab.
  • FMEA: severity, occurrence and detection in D:F, =PRODUCT(D2:F2) in G2, fill down, then SORTBY to list the worst first.
  • Watch severity on its own: a severity of 10 needs action even when the RPN is low. COUNTIFS flags those rows.
\[ \text{RPN} = S \times O \times D \]

where \(S\) is severity, \(O\) is occurrence and \(D\) is detection, each rated 1 to 10.

Minitab navigation

  • Compute RPN: Calc → Calculator, store the result in a new column, expression S * O * D.
  • Pareto of RPN by failure mode: Stat → Quality Tools → Pareto Chart. Choose Chart defects table, labels = failure mode, frequencies = RPN.
  • Alternative: Graph → Bar Chart with values from a table, if you want the bars unsorted.
  • FMEA forms: Minitab Statistical Software has no FMEA or fault-tree menu. Minitab Workspace, a separate product, has FMEA, fishbone and Five Whys forms. Build gate maths in Calc → Calculator or Excel.

Exam tips

  • Deductive = top-down = FTA. Inductive = bottom-up = FMEA. Event tree analysis is also inductive.
  • AND gates multiply. Expect a question that asks for the top event from two or three basic-event probabilities.
  • Bow-tie = a fault tree on the left plus an event tree on the right.
  • HAZOP identifies, HAZAN assesses. HAZID comes first, at the concept or layout stage.
  • HAZOP = guide words × parameters at each node of a P&ID.
  • RPN ranks risks but hides severity. The AIAG-VDA FMEA handbook uses Action Priority (AP) instead.
