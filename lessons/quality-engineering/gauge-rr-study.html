Quality Engineering · Measurement System Analysis · Beginner

Gage R&R
You Can See

A measurement is never just a number. Learn to see what came from the part, what came from the gage, and what came from the person using it—then run the study yourself.

75 minutesBeginner8 live activitiesMinitab + Excel
By the end: you will be able to explain, design, run, interpret, and improve a variable Gage R&R study without treating one percentage as the whole answer.

What should create the spread?

Real partsLarge
RepeatabilitySmall
ReproducibilitySmall

A good system makes real part differences easy to see and measurement noise hard to notice.

Your learning route

Start With the Question, Not the Formula

1 · See the errorMove sliders and watch repeated readings separate, shift, or drift.
2 · Name the sourceTell repeatability, reproducibility, bias, linearity, stability, and part variation apart.
3 · Run the studyCollect a randomized crossed study and watch ANOVA separate the components.
4 · Read the evidenceUse graphs, %Study Variation, %Tolerance, %Contribution, and ndc together.
5 · Choose the designSelect crossed, nested, expanded, Type 1, or linearity and bias based on the question.
6 · Improve the systemConnect the dominant error source to practical corrective action.

Vocabulary note: industry software and the AIAG manual commonly spell the word gage. General English often uses gauge. This lesson uses “Gage R&R,” but both spellings refer to the same study.

01 · First principles

Every Reading Has a Story

Suppose a pipe wall is truly 8.000 mm thick. A micrometer might show 8.006 mm. The extra 0.006 mm did not necessarily come from the pipe.

Observed reading = reference or part value + systematic error + random error

Systematic Error and Random Error Are Different

Systematic

Shift or pattern

The results are consistently high, low, changing with size, or drifting with time. Bias, linearity, and stability problems live here.

It can be precise and still wrong.

Random

Scatter

The result moves unpredictably when the same thing is measured again. Repeatability and reproducibility are major sources of this scatter.

It makes real differences harder to see.

Accuracy and Precision Are Not Synonyms

Accurate and precise: readings are close to the reference and close to one another. This is the goal.

Gage R&R mainly studies precision. Bias and linearity studies assess accuracy against reference values. A complete MSA needs both questions.

02 · The MSA family

Six Concepts Beginners Must Separate

Select a card. Each concept asks a different question and needs different evidence.

Repeatability (equipment variation, EV)

Question: If one appraiser measures the same part several times under the same conditions, how tightly do the readings agree?

Evidence: repeated readings, within-cell ranges, the R chart, and the repeatability variance component.

Typical action: inspect gage condition, fixturing, locating method, measurement force, environment, and resolution.

Where Part Variation Fits

Part-to-part variation (PV) is the useful signal. It is the real variation among the sampled parts. Gage R&R asks whether the measurement system can separate that signal from measurement noise.

Repeatability

Scatter when the same appraiser repeats the measurement.

Reproducibility

Differences among appraisers, plus part × appraiser interaction when included.

Total Gage R&R

The combined measurement-system standard deviation.

\(\sigma_{GRR}=\sqrt{\sigma^2_{repeatability}+\sigma^2_{appraiser}+\sigma^2_{part\times appraiser}}\) \(\sigma_{total}=\sqrt{\sigma^2_{GRR}+\sigma^2_{part}}\)

Do not add standard deviations directly. Independent variance components add. Convert back to standard deviation by taking the square root.

03 · Accuracy across size and time

Bias, Linearity, and Stability—Made Visible

Bias and Linearity Lab

Bias is the average measurement minus the accepted reference value. Positive bias means the gage reads high; negative bias means it reads low. Linearity asks whether that bias stays the same across the operating range.

04 · Choose the right study

Study Design Comes Before Software

First verify the basics: the instrument is calibrated, its resolution is suitable, the normal work method is defined, the characteristic is measurable, and the study parts are safe and representative. Gage R&R cannot repair a flawed study design.

Pick a situation

Crossed Gage R&R

Every appraiser measures every part. Use this for non-destructive measurements such as wall thickness on the same pipe rings.

Structure: Part × Appraiser × Trial

A Defensible Crossed Study—Step by Step

  1. Define the decision. State the characteristic, units, specification, intended use, and required measurement performance.
  2. Select representative parts. Use at least about 10 parts spanning the normal low-to-high process range—not ten nearly identical parts.
  3. Select typical appraisers. A common starting point is three people representing normal shifts, skill levels, and methods.
  4. Plan repeats. Use two or three trials per appraiser-part cell. A 10 × 3 × 2 design gives 60 readings.
  5. Randomize and blind. Hide part identity where practical, randomize order for every trial, and keep earlier results hidden.
  6. Reset the measurement. Remove, reposition, and re-fixture the part between repeats. Re-reading the same display understates error.
  7. Use normal conditions. Keep the production method, fixture, environment, instrument, and rounding rule realistic.
  8. Record—not erase—events. Document unusual conditions. Investigate questionable readings rather than silently deleting them.

Destructive-test warning: if appraisers do not measure the same physical parts, the ordinary crossed model is not present. Use a justified nested or expanded design; do not relabel different specimens as the “same part.”

Challenge 1

Which graph exposes poor repeatability?

Load the poor-repeatability preset. Predict what changes before showing the answer.

Within-part points spread vertically, appraiser boxes widen, and repeatability dominates the variance chart. The interaction lines may remain broadly parallel.
Challenge 2

What does crossing mean?

Load interaction problem. Why can similar overall appraiser averages still mislead?

Crossing or fanning lines mean an appraiser’s difference depends on the part. Overall averages can cancel while part-specific disagreement remains.
Challenge 3

Why can narrow parts hurt ndc?

Load narrow part range. The gage did not change—so why did the study look worse?

The sampled PV shrank. The same measurement noise now occupies more of total study variation, so %Study Variation rises and ndc falls. This is a sampling problem, not automatically a damaged gage.
06 · Interpret without shortcuts

Four Metrics, Four Different Questions

MetricQuestion answeredCommon guidelineImportant caution
%Study VariationHow large is measurement spread relative to the spread in this study?<10% acceptable; 10–30% conditional; >30% generally unacceptableDepends strongly on whether study parts represent the intended process variation.
%ToleranceHow much of USL − LSL is consumed by 6σ of measurement error?Same common 10% and 30% bandsNeeds the correct full tolerance and the chosen study-variation multiplier.
%ContributionWhat share of total variance comes from each source?<1% acceptable; 1–9% conditional; >9% generally unacceptable for Total Gage R&RUses variance, so it is the squared counterpart of the study-variation proportion.
ndcHow many distinct part categories can the system separate?Common minimum ≥5; ≥10 is strongerA narrow part sample can reduce ndc even when repeatability is unchanged.

These are guidelines, not universal laws. Customer rules, decision risk, product safety, cost, and intended use can require stricter criteria. State the governing criterion in the report.

Core Formulas

%Study Variation

\(100\times\sigma_{GRR}/\sigma_{total}\)

Compares measurement spread with the observed study spread.

%Tolerance

\(100\times 6\sigma_{GRR}/(USL-LSL)\)

Uses the full tolerance width. Minitab’s default study-variation multiplier is 6.

%Contribution

\(100\times\sigma^2_{GRR}/\sigma^2_{total}\)

Compares variance rather than standard deviation.

Distinct categories

\(ndc=1.41\times\sigma_{part}/\sigma_{GRR}\)

Software reports an integer category count.

Read the Graphs Before the Verdict

  1. Start with the R chart or within-cell ranges. Unstable or inconsistent repeats make later averages less trustworthy.
  2. Check measurements by part. Repeats should cluster tightly; part averages should cover the intended range.
  3. Check measurements by appraiser. Look for shifted centres, unequal spread, rounding patterns, or one appraiser behaving differently.
  4. Check part × appraiser interaction. Crossing or fanning lines mean disagreement depends on the part.
  5. Then read variance components and metrics. Identify the dominant measurement source and connect it to action.
07 · Worked steel example

Pipe-Wall Micrometer Study

A quality team studies a 0.500 mm-wide tolerance using 10 pipe rings, 3 inspectors, and 2 randomized trials. ANOVA estimates:

Repeatability σ0.018 mmwithin-appraiser scatter
Appraiser σ0.012 mmbetween-appraiser effect
Part σ0.110 mmreal sampled part spread
\(\sigma_{GRR}=\sqrt{0.018^2+0.012^2}=0.0216\text{ mm}\) \(\%StudyVar=100\times0.0216/\sqrt{0.0216^2+0.110^2}=19.3\%\) \(\%Tolerance=100\times6(0.0216)/0.500=26.0\%\) \(ndc=\lfloor1.41(0.110/0.0216)\rfloor=7\)
Conclusion: the system is conditionally acceptable under common guidelines, not automatically “passed.” It can distinguish at least five categories, but measurement error consumes a meaningful share of both study variation and tolerance. Repeatability is the larger component, so first investigate measurement force, contact cleanliness, positioning, fixturing, and instrument condition. Confirm the applicable customer criterion before release.
08 · Software implementation

Run It in Minitab or Build Carefully in Excel

Minitab · Crossed

Stat → Quality Tools → Gage Study → Gage R&R Study (Crossed)
  1. Place one reading per row in Part, Appraiser, and Measurement columns.
  2. Assign the matching columns to Part numbers, Operators, and Measurement data.
  3. Select ANOVA as the method.
  4. In Options, enter USL − LSL as process tolerance and confirm the study-variation multiplier.
  5. Request confidence intervals when decisions are high risk.

Minitab · Other designs

Stat → Quality Tools → Gage Study
  • Gage R&R Study (Nested): parts are unique to appraisers.
  • Gage R&R Study (Expanded): additional factors, fixed factors, crossed/nested mixtures, or unbalanced data.
  • Type 1 Gage Study: one reference part, one appraiser, repeated measurements.
  • Gage Linearity and Bias Study: accuracy across reference values.

Six Traps That Ruin Conclusions

Narrow part range

Understates PV, inflates %Study Variation, and reduces ndc.

Wrong design

Crossed analysis cannot represent destructive measurements on unique specimens.

No repositioning

Repeated display readings make repeatability look artificially small.

Adding SDs

Variance components add; standard deviations combine by root-sum-of-squares.

Ignoring graphs

A single Total Gage R&R value can hide interaction or an abnormal appraiser.

Equating precision with accuracy

A highly repeatable gage can still be biased or nonlinear.

Practice case: Should this system be released?

A study reports %Study Variation = 8%, %Tolerance = 7%, and ndc = 12. A separate reference study shows +0.080 mm bias on a ±0.100 mm specification.

Answer: Do not release it based on Gage R&R alone. Precision is strong, but the systematic offset is large relative to the specification. Investigate and correct bias, then verify accuracy and repeatability again.

Practice case: Is a 35% Gage R&R always a gage failure?

Answer: It is generally unacceptable under common guidelines, but first check the study design. If all parts came from a very narrow range, PV may be understated. %Tolerance, graphs, sample representativeness, and the dominant variance component tell you whether the main problem is the system, the sample, or both.

11 · Final mental model

The Whole Lesson in One Minute

Signal

PV is the real difference among study parts. You want the gage to separate it clearly.

Random noise

Repeatability + reproducibility + interaction combine into Total Gage R&R.

Systematic error

Bias, linearity, and stability can make precise results consistently wrong.

  • Define the measurement decision and choose the correct study.
  • Use representative parts, normal appraisers, repeats, randomization, blinding, and repositioning.
  • Inspect graphs before reducing the study to one number.
  • Interpret %Study Variation, %Tolerance, %Contribution, and ndc as different views—not interchangeable labels.
  • Match the dominant source to corrective action, then repeat the study under the improved method.
  • Never claim accuracy, linearity, or stability from Gage R&R alone.
