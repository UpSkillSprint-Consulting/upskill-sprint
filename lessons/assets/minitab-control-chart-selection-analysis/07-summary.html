Final summary

Key takeaways

  • Choose the chart from the data-generating process, not from preference.
  • Continuous subgroup data use Xbar-R, Xbar-S, or I-MR-R/S.
  • Continuous individual data usually use I-MR; mixed short runs may require Z-MR.
  • Defectives use P/NP/Laney P′; defects use C/U/Laney U′.
  • EWMA and CUSUM improve sensitivity to small sustained shifts.
  • T², Generalized Variance, and MEWMA monitor correlated variables jointly.
  • G and T charts monitor spacing between rare events.
  • Stability must be established before capability is interpreted.

Concise decision rule

Measure or count? Individual or subgroup? Defective or defect? Constant or varying opportunity? One variable or correlated variables? Common shift or rare event?

Remember this: choose the chart from the data structure, then read variation before location.
