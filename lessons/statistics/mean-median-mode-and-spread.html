Mean, Median, Mode and Spread in 15 Minutes

The mean, median and mode tell you where your data sits. Spread tells you how far it wanders.

Part 1: center

Mean, median and mode: three ways to find the middle

The same seven repair times as the poster above.

The people-pleaser

Mean: the balance point

“One outlier walks in and I change my whole answer.”

Add every value, then divide by how many there are.

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n} = \frac{147}{7} = 21 \]

where \(x_i\) is each value and \(n\) is the number of values.

=AVERAGE()

The diplomat

Median: the middle value

“Sort it, split it, done. Your outlier is not my problem.”

Sort the values and take the middle one. With an even count, average the two middle values.

\[ \tilde{x} = 18 \]

The 4th of 7 sorted values.

=MEDIAN()

The popular kid

Mode: the most common value

“I don't do math. I do headcounts.”

Count which value appears most often. It works for categories too.

\[ \text{Mode} = 15 \]

15 appears twice; every other value once.

=MODE.SNGL()

Part 2: choosing

Which center should you report?

  1. Are the values categories, such as defect types or 1 to 5 ratings?

    YesUse the mode. Ratings can also use the median.

    ↓ No: next question

  2. Is the data skewed, or does it have outliers?

    YesUse the median and IQR.

    ↓ No: next question

  3. OtherwiseUse the mean and standard deviation.

Not sure? A big gap between the mean and the median means skew.

Part 3: spread

Range, standard deviation, IQR and CV: measuring spread

The drama queen

Range

33 h

Largest minus smallest. Fast, but one extreme value controls it.

\[ R = 45 - 12 = 33 \]

The typical wanderer

Standard deviation

11.1 h

The typical distance from the mean, in the data's own units.

\[ s = 11.1 \]

The bouncer

Interquartile range

7 h

The spread of the middle 50%. Outliers cannot move it.

\[ Q_3 - Q_1 = 22 - 15 = 7 \]

The translator

Coefficient of variation

52.9%

Standard deviation as a percentage of the mean. Compares spread across scales.

\[ \frac{s}{\bar{x}} \times 100\% = 52.9\% \]

\[ \begin{aligned} s^2 &= \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1} = \frac{740}{6} = 123.3 \\ s &= \sqrt{123.3} = 11.1 \end{aligned} \]

where \(s^2\) is the sample variance and \(s\) the sample standard deviation.

77.8% of the squared distance comes from the 45-hour repair.

The IQR on a box plot

\[ \text{Upper fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 22 + 1.5 \times 7 = 32.5 \]

4 mistakes to avoid

  • ✕Mean of skewed dataA long tail drags it. Report the median.
  • ✕Averaging ratings1 to 5 scores are ordinal. Use the median or mode.
  • ✕Adding standard deviationsAdd variances, then take the square root.
  • ✕STDEV.P on a sampleSamples divide by \(n - 1\): use STDEV.S.

Excel use cases

Put the seven repair times in cells A2:A8.

TaskFormulaResult
Mean=AVERAGE(A2:A8)21
Median=MEDIAN(A2:A8)18
Mode=MODE.SNGL(A2:A8)15
Range=MAX(A2:A8)-MIN(A2:A8)33
Sample variance=VAR.S(A2:A8)123.33
Sample standard deviation=STDEV.S(A2:A8)11.11
Population standard deviation=STDEV.P(A2:A8)10.28
IQR=QUARTILE.EXC(A2:A8,3)-QUARTILE.EXC(A2:A8,1)7
CV=STDEV.S(A2:A8)/AVERAGE(A2:A8)52.9% (format as %)

Exam tips

  • Sample or population?Unless the question says population, divide by \(n - 1\).
  • Skew questionsGiven mean, median and mode, the mean points to the tail.
  • Combining sourcesAdd variances: \(\sigma_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2}\).
  • Control chartsEstimate sigma from ranges: \(\hat{\sigma} = \bar{R} / d_2\).
  • Different unitsCompare variability with CV, not standard deviation.
