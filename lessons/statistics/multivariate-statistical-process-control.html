Prerequisites: basic means, standard deviations and control limits. All examples use synthetic teaching data, not production records.

Before the mathematics: why do we need multivariate SPC?

Univariate SPC monitors one characteristic at a time. Multivariate SPC monitors several related characteristics as a vector. A process can look normal on each separate chart while its usual relationship between characteristics changes.

Neither picture determines whether a pipe meets its engineering specifications. Statistical control and product acceptance are separate decisions.

The hidden signal: two variables, one story

Yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) often move together. A pipe with unusually high YS and low UTS may be suspicious even if each value is within its own control limits. Control limits describe statistical behaviour, not engineering specifications.

Essential vocabulary

Vector

A row of paired measurements, not separate unrelated samples.

Covariance

How two deviations move together, in product units.

Correlation

Standardized covariance, between \(-1\) and \(+1\).

Rational subgroup

Measurements collected under comparable short-term conditions.

ARL

Expected number of plotted points before a signal.

Decomposition

A diagnostic contribution analysis, not proof of causality.

Covariance: the process fingerprint

A variance describes the spread of one variable. Covariance describes how two variables move together. Correlation scales covariance to a unitless number between \(-1\) and \(+1\).

100

YS variance (MPa²)

80

YS–UTS covariance (MPa²)

0.80

Correlation

\[S=\begin{bmatrix}100&80\\80&100\end{bmatrix},\quad r=\frac{80}{10(10)}=0.8\]

The diagonal entries are variances; off-diagonal entries are covariances. Both standard deviations equal 10 MPa. Positive covariance means the variables tend to move in the same direction.

Calculate it from measurements

For paired observations, the sample covariance is \[s_{xy}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{N-1}\] where \(N\) is the number of paired observations and the bars represent sample means. Replacing \(y\) with \(x\) gives the sample variance.

For YS values 480, 490, 500, 510, 520 and UTS values 580, 595, 590, 615, 620, the means are 500 and 600. The sums of squared deviations are 1000 and 1150; cross-products total 1000.

The fixed matrix in the saved project is a teaching assumption. When you monitor an actual production process, estimate the mean and covariance from a representative, stable baseline. \[S_{\mathrm{pool}}=\begin{bmatrix}98.6679&88.3904\\88.3904&114.0702\end{bmatrix}\]

Why pool the individual subgroup matrices?

\[S_{\mathrm{pool}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(n_i-1)S_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(n_i-1)}\]

For equal sizes, simply average the subgroup covariance matrices. For this example the denominator is \(40 \times 9 = 360\). The pooled estimate describes short-term variation, while covariance across all 400 readings can include between-subgroup movements.

01 · Joint location

Hotelling's T²: detect a joint mean shift

\[T_i^2=n(\bar{\mathbf x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_0)^T S^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_0)\]

Here \(n\) is subgroup size, \(\bar{\mathbf x}_i\) the subgroup mean vector, \(\boldsymbol\mu_0\) the reference mean vector, and \(S\) the reference covariance matrix. The inverse covariance adjusts the distance for natural spread and relationships.

A superscript T means transpose: it turns a column into a row. The superscript \(-1\) denotes the inverse matrix, not the reciprocal of each entry. Multiply the row of mean deviations, the inverse covariance, and the column of deviations in that order; the result is one number.

For one observation and two variables with standardized deviations \(z_1,z_2\) and correlation \(r\): \[T^2=\frac{z_1^2-2rz_1z_2+z_2^2}{1-r^2}\]

Pipe A gives 2.5 and Pipe B gives 22.5 when both standard deviations are 10 MPa and correlation is 0.8. With known population parameters the theoretical limit is a chi-square quantile; estimated parameters require chart-specific limits. The saved Minitab UCL is 27.1452, not the illustrative 11.83 limit used for the individual-observation lab.

▸ Why the upper control limits differ

For a known mean and known covariance, independent normal subgroup means have \(T^2\sim\chi_p^2\). For \(p=2\) and upper-tail probability 0.0027, the quantile is 11.829. This is the introductory ellipse threshold. The saved chart instead uses Minitab's historical-sample-count setting of 2 and returns 27.1452. Use that number to reproduce the saved project, not as a general production recommendation.

When the parameters are estimated, the reference sample size, the estimator and whether the plotted observations were used to estimate the baseline affect the distribution.

Phase I and Phase II limits need not match. For a new production chart use Minitab's documented formula for that design rather than copying either exercise limit. The MEWMA limit also depends on the weight and run-length target.

02 · Joint dispersion

Generalized Variance: detect joint dispersion

\[GV_i=\det(S_i)=s_1^2s_2^2(1-r^2)\]

For two variables, \(s_1,s_2\) are subgroup standard deviations and \(r\) is their subgroup correlation. The determinant measures joint dispersion, not the arithmetic sum of variances.

3,600

Both SDs 10; correlation 0.8

18,225

Both SDs 15; correlation 0.8

9,600

Both SDs 10; correlation 0.2

Even when each standard deviation is unchanged, weaker correlation can increase generalized variance. Conversely, different covariance matrices can have the same determinant. A sign reversal of correlation alone is invisible to this statistic.

04 · Persistent shift

MEWMA: remember the past

\[\mathbf Z_i=\lambda(\bar{\mathbf x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_0)+(1-\lambda)\mathbf Z_{i-1}\]

Start with a zero vector. The weight \(\lambda\) determines how much the newest subgroup contributes. Smaller weights retain more history.

\[\Sigma_{Z_i}=\frac{\lambda}{2-\lambda}(1-(1-\lambda)^{2i})\frac{S}{n}\]

\[M_i=\mathbf Z_i^T\Sigma_{Z_i}^{-1}\mathbf Z_i\]

These formulas assume independent equal-sized subgroups and fixed reference covariance. The weighted vector's covariance changes during startup. MEWMA limits are calibrated for run-length performance rather than borrowed from T².

Behind the chart: calculate the statistics yourself

Hotelling's T² — why the inverse matrix matters

Think of the matrix as a map of normal directions of movement. For positively correlated strengths, moving both upward is less unusual than moving one upward and the other downward by the same amount.

\[S^{-1}=\frac{1}{3600}\begin{bmatrix}100&-80\\-80&100\end{bmatrix}\]

The determinant is 3600. For Pipe B the deviation vector is \((15,-15)^T\). Multiplication yields:

\[T^2=\frac{100(15)^2-160(15)(-15)+100(-15)^2}{3600}=22.5\]

For Pipe A, both deviations are positive and the cross term subtracts rather than adds, yielding 2.5. The same two absolute deviations can produce a ninefold difference in squared distance.

Generalized Variance — from subgroup measurements to a control limit

Suppose the within-subgroup covariance is the reference matrix. Its determinant is:

\[100(100)-80^2=3600\]

If both standard deviations grow by 50% while correlation remains 0.8, the variances become 225 and covariance becomes 180. The determinant becomes 18225. Doubling both standard deviations would multiply the determinant by 16 for two variables.

For independent multivariate normal subgroups of size \(n\), with \(p\) variables and population covariance \(\Sigma\), the conventional unadjusted determinant has mean and variance:

\[\begin{aligned}E(|S_i|)&=b_1|\Sigma|\\ \operatorname{Var}(|S_i|)&=b_2|\Sigma|^2\end{aligned}\]

\[b_1=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(n-j)}{(n-1)^p}\]

\[b_2=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(n-j)}{(n-1)^{2p}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}(n-j+2)-\prod_{j=1}^{p}(n-j)\right]\]

Moment-based limits for the raw determinant use \(b_1|\Sigma|\pm3\sqrt{b_2}|\Sigma|\), truncated at zero. A chart using the reference determinant as its center line can scale the relative standard deviation differently; see the specific Minitab calculation below. Always match the plotted statistic and the software's estimator to its limit formula.

Reproduce Minitab's Generalized Variance control limits

The preceding raw-determinant moments are useful for understanding the sampling distribution. Minitab's historical-covariance GV convention uses the reference determinant as its center line and scales the relative standard deviation by the bias factor:

\[\mathrm{CL}=|S|,\quad \mathrm{UCL}=|S|\left(1+3\frac{\sqrt{b_2}}{b_1}\right),\quad \mathrm{LCL}=\max\left(0,|S|\left(1-3\frac{\sqrt{b_2}}{b_1}\right)\right).\]

For two variables and 10 observations per subgroup, the constants are \(b_1=8/9\) and \(b_2=2736/6561\). With reference determinant 3600, these yield CL = 3600, UCL = 11,446.0181 and LCL = 0, exactly matching the saved Minitab output. The plotted points themselves are raw sample determinants, not divided by the bias factor. Minitab's GV methods and formulas.

Control limits are not interchangeable

For known population parameters and multivariate normal subgroup means, \(T^2\) has a chi-square distribution with \(p\) degrees of freedom. Estimated-parameter charts may require F or beta limits depending on Phase I or Phase II and the estimation method. MEWMA needs its own run-length-calibrated UCL.

Do not compare detection delays between charts unless their false-alarm behaviour is comparable. A faster alarm in a single dataset is not proof of universal superiority.

Which control chart should I use?

One variable

Shewhart for current means or spread; CUSUM and EWMA for persistent mean changes.

Several variables

T² for joint location; GV for dispersion; combined for both; MEWMA for persistent shifts.

Shewhart plots the current value; CUSUM accumulates standardized deviations beyond a reference allowance; EWMA weights the current and previous values. T² and MEWMA additionally account for covariance.

\[C_i^+=\max(0,C_{i-1}^++y_i-k)\]

Here \(y_i\) is a standardized observation and \(k\) is a reference allowance. Compare the statistic to a decision interval. The lower CUSUM accumulates negative deviations analogously.

\[E_i=\lambda x_i+(1-\lambda)E_{i-1}\]

EWMA uses observation \(x_i\) and smoothing weight \(\lambda\). For fair comparisons, calibrate charts to comparable in-control average run lengths.

Reproduce the saved results, one subgroup at a time

Compare the ten-point clouds using the same axes. Moving the cloud's center and spreading the cloud apart are distinct disturbances.

Subgroup 41 — a mean shift in opposite directions

The actual subgroup averages are 510.0000 MPa and 590.0000 MPa. With subgroup size 10 and reference means 500 and 600, the deviations are +10 and −10. Inserting these into the covariance-adjusted distance gives:

\[T^2=10\frac{100(10)^2-160(10)(-10)+100(-10)^2}{3600}=100.\]

Compare 100 with 27.1452: investigate. The dispersion statistic is 3148.86, below 11446.02, so this particular signal is a location disturbance without a determinant signal.

Subgroup 46 — spread changes, not the mean

Calculate each sample variance and covariance from the ten paired rows (divide by nine). The entries below come from the original worksheet.

\[S_{46}=\begin{bmatrix}384.767&410.554\\410.554&561.262\end{bmatrix}\]

Its determinant is 47400.30 MPa⁴, versus UCL 11446.02. Yet its \(T^2\) is only 3.6050. A conventional location chart could miss this dispersion disturbance.

Subgroup 51 — both panels demand attention

Actual mean YS: 510.0000 MPa; actual mean UTS: 590.0000 MPa. \(T^2 = 99.9999\) and \(\text{GV} = 135264.98\). Check measurement integrity and spread first; then investigate the process shift.

Subgroup 45 — a memory chart crosses its threshold

The weighted deviation vector at 45 is (0.210616, -2.044738) MPa. Apply the startup covariance multiplier to the reference covariance of the subgroup mean, then compute the quadratic form. The result is 12.285905, exceeding 9.647594. The preceding point is 6.533482. The first signal is 45, not the first moment a change may have started.

Investigate, don't just alarm

01Verify measurement
02Inspect individual charts
03Check covariance and decomposition
04Confirm process cause

Look for gauge problems, changes in grade or product mix, process settings, and operator or equipment changes. Decomposition helps identify variables that contribute to the signal conditional on the others; it does not prove causation.

Other applications: monitor chemical reactor temperature, pressure and concentration; mining recovery and feed grade; finance risk factors; or dimensional characteristics such as diameter and ovality. Avoid redundant variables that make the covariance matrix singular. Use representative data, independent observations, sufficient sample size and appropriate distribution assumptions.

2. Excel Use Cases

For paired samples in B2:B6 and C2:C6, use =VAR.S(B2:B6), =COVARIANCE.S(B2:B6,C2:C6) and =CORREL(B2:B6,C2:C6). Put the covariance matrix in F2:G3 and a row of mean deviations in I2:J2. 4. Exam Tips

  • The ASQ CSSBB and CQE bodies of knowledge do not name Hotelling’s \(T^2\), generalized variance or MEWMA charts. Treat this lesson as beyond-the-exam depth, and know the ideas the exams do test.
  • Covariance and correlation: the diagonal of a covariance matrix holds variances, and correlation is covariance scaled to between \(-1\) and \(+1\).
  • Control limits are not specification limits. A signal means “investigate”, never “reject the product”.
  • Rational subgroups and Phase I versus Phase II: build limits from a stable baseline, then freeze them to monitor new data.
  • Location versus dispersion: a means chart and a spread chart answer different questions, just like \(T^2\) and GV here.
Practice / Think like the analyst

Can you solve the case before opening Minitab?

Case A: the unusual combination

Historical means 500 and 600 MPa. Both standard deviations 10 MPa, correlation 0.8. A new individual pipe measures 510 and 590 MPa. Calculate its T² and compare with the illustrative limit 11.83.

Reveal worked solution

The standardized deviations are \(+1\) and \(-1\), so \(T^2 = 10\). This does not exceed 11.83, although the combination is more unusual than both measurements rising together.

Case B: the spread changes

Both standard deviations increase from 10 to 20 MPa with correlation unchanged at 0.8. By what factor does GV change?

Reveal worked solution

Each variance quadruples. The determinant grows sixteenfold, from 3600 to 57600. This is a dispersion question, not a mean-shift question.

Case C: the drift continues

A series of individually modest joint deviations persists. Which chart should you add and what parameter controls memory?

Reveal worked solution

MEWMA. Lambda controls the newest observation weight. A lower lambda generally emphasizes persistent changes, but limits must be recalibrated.
