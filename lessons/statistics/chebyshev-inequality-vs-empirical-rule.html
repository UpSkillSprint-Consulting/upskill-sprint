Chebyshev vs. the Empirical Rule

Learn two different ways to describe how much data lies near the mean—and why one is a guarantee for almost any distribution while the other is a stronger approximation for bell-shaped data.

Why this matters: engineers and analysts often know only a mean and standard deviation. Choosing the wrong rule can make a cautious statement look falsely precise.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Explain the difference between a guaranteed lower bound and a normal-distribution approximation.
  2. Calculate Chebyshev coverage and the interval μ ± kσ.
  3. Compare Chebyshev with the 68–95–99.7 Empirical Rule.
  4. Select the appropriate rule from the information given about distribution shape.
  5. Interpret results using “at least,” “at most,” and “approximately” correctly.

Prerequisites

No prior knowledge is required. It helps to know that the mean is the average and standard deviation measures typical spread around that average.

The idea in plain English

Chebyshev's inequality

Gives a minimum guaranteed proportion inside a symmetric interval for any distribution with a finite mean and variance—even when the shape is skewed, bimodal, or unknown.

The Empirical Rule

States that approximately 68%, 95%, and 99.7% lie within 1, 2, and 3 standard deviations when data are approximately normal.

What neither rule tells you: neither proves a process is stable, capable, or normal. They describe spread around the mean, not conformity to specifications.

Key terminology

Mean, μ

The population average and centre of the interval.

Standard deviation, σ

The population's typical spread, in the same units as the data.

k

The number of standard deviations placed on each side of the mean.

Coverage

The proportion of observations inside μ ± kσ.

Lower bound

A minimum that the true coverage cannot fall below under the stated assumptions.

Approximately normal

Roughly symmetric, single-peaked, and bell-shaped without severe outliers.

Core formulas

P(|X − μ| < kσ) ≥ 1 − 1k²

Chebyshev's inside bound, valid for k > 1.

Lower = μ − kσ   |   Upper = μ + kσ

The bounds use the same units as the original measurement.

Maximum outside ≤ 1k²

Inside and outside are complements: inside ≥ 1 − 1/k²; outside ≤ 1/k².

Assumptions: Chebyshev requires a finite mean and finite variance. The Empirical Rule additionally requires a distribution reasonably close to normal.

Step-by-step method

  1. Identify the question. Are you finding a guaranteed percentage, a normal-model percentage, or numerical bounds?
  2. Check distribution information. If shape is unknown or clearly non-normal, use Chebyshev. If approximately normal, the Empirical Rule may be used.
  3. Determine k. From an interval, compute k = distance from mean ÷ standard deviation.
  4. Calculate coverage. Chebyshev uses 1 − 1/k². Normal data use the Empirical Rule or exact normal probabilities.
  5. Calculate bounds. Use μ − kσ and μ + kσ.
  6. Interpret with disciplined language. Say “at least” for Chebyshev and “approximately” for the Empirical Rule.
  7. Document the assumption. State whether distribution shape was unknown, non-normal, or approximately normal.

Worked example: package fill weights

Problem: A filler produces packages with mean 500 g and standard deviation 12 g. The distribution shape has not been established. What can be guaranteed within two standard deviations?

Select the method. Shape is unknown, so use Chebyshev.
Set k. The question says two standard deviations, so k = 2.
Calculate coverage. 1 − 1/2² = 1 − 1/4 = 0.75 = 75%.
Calculate bounds. 500 ± 2(12) = 500 ± 24, giving 476 g to 524 g.
Interpret. At least 75% of all package weights lie between 476 g and 524 g, assuming the population has a finite mean and variance.
Limitation: you cannot claim 95% unless the distribution is reasonably normal. Chebyshev's 75% is conservative; actual coverage may be higher.

Common mistakes and exam traps

Calculation error

Using 1 − 1/k

Why wrong: k must be squared. Avoid it: write 1 − 1/k² before substituting.

Interpretation error

Saying “exactly 75%”

Why wrong: Chebyshev gives a minimum. Avoid it: always say “at least.”

Assumption error

Using 68–95–99.7 for any shape

Why wrong: the Empirical Rule depends on approximate normality. Avoid it: check shape first.

Calculation error

Forgetting the numerical interval

Why wrong: a percentage alone does not identify the measurement range. Avoid it: calculate μ ± kσ.

Software-output error

Using NORM.DIST as Chebyshev

Why wrong: Excel's normal functions assume a normal model. Avoid it: calculate Chebyshev directly.

Exam trap

Using k = 1 as a useful bound

Why wrong: Chebyshev gives 0% at k = 1. Avoid it: use k > 1.

Excel and Minitab guidance

Excel

With mean in B2, standard deviation in B3, and k in B4:

  • Lower: =B2-B4*B3
  • Upper: =B2+B4*B3
  • Chebyshev inside: =1-1/B4^2
  • Maximum outside: =1/B4^2
  • Exact normal area: =NORM.S.DIST(B4,TRUE)-NORM.S.DIST(-B4,TRUE)
Caution: normal functions do not verify normality.

Minitab

  1. Assess shape with Graph → Histogram or Graph → Probability Plot.
  2. Use Stat → Basic Statistics → Graphical Summary for mean, standard deviation, and normality information.
  3. Use Calc → Calculator to store 1 - 1 / k**2 when k is in a worksheet constant or column.
  4. Report the rule, k, bounds, and distribution assumption.

Minitab supports the analysis; it does not choose the correct theorem for you.

Practice activity

A filling process has mean 250 mL and standard deviation 4 mL. Distribution shape is unknown. Find an interval guaranteed to contain at least 90% of fills.

Set 1 − 1/k² = 0.90. Then 1/k² = 0.10, so k² = 10 and k = √10 = 3.162.

Bounds: 250 ± 3.162(4) = 250 ± 12.649, or approximately 237.35 mL to 262.65 mL.

Conclusion: at least 90% of fills lie within this interval, assuming finite mean and variance.

Final summary

  • Chebyshev gives a minimum guaranteed coverage for any distribution with finite mean and variance.
  • The Empirical Rule gives approximate 68–95–99.7 coverage only for approximately normal data.
  • Chebyshev inside coverage is 1 − 1/k² for k > 1.
  • Numerical bounds are μ − kσ and μ + kσ.
  • Use “at least” for Chebyshev and “approximately” for the Empirical Rule.
  • A conservative bound can be much lower than the actual proportion.
Decision rule: unknown or non-normal shape → Chebyshev; approximately normal shape → Empirical Rule or exact normal probabilities.
Remember this: Chebyshev guarantees; the Empirical Rule assumes.
