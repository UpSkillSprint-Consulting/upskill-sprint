Upload or paste your own x–y data. The finder fits every model family from the transformation lesson, from the 64 rungs of the ladder of powers to Box–Cox, Box–Tidwell, nonlinear, hyperbolic-tangent and GLM models, and ranks them on criteria that are fair across different y scales.

  1. Load data and pick the x and y columns.
  2. Press Find best linear model for the single best straight-line transformation, or fit and rank every model by AICc, cross-validated error or R².

      How the ranking works

      Every model is scored on y itself. A model fitted to \(g(y)\) assumes normal errors on that scale. Its log-likelihood for the original y adds the Jacobian of the transformation, which is what makes AICc comparable across \(y\), \(\ln y\), \(\sqrt{y}\) and the rest:

      \[ \ell_{y} = -\frac{n}{2}\left[\ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi\,\text{SSE}_{g}}{n}\right) + 1\right] + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ln\left|g'(y_i)\right| \]

      where \(\text{SSE}_{g}\) is the error sum of squares on the fitting scale and \(n\) is the number of points.

      \[ \text{AICc} = -2\ell_{y} + 2k + \frac{2k(k+1)}{n-k-1}, \qquad w_m = \frac{e^{-\Delta_m/2}}{\sum_j e^{-\Delta_j/2}} \]

      where \(k\) counts every estimated parameter including the error variance, \(\Delta_m\) is a model's AICc minus the lowest AICc, and \(w_m\) is its Akaike weight. Models within \(\Delta < 2\) are effectively tied: prefer the simplest one, or the one with a physical meaning. The Poisson GLM has a discrete likelihood, so its AICc is shown but not ranked against the continuous models; compare it by cross-validated RMSE.

      Cross-validated RMSE refits each model on 9 of 10 folds and predicts the held-out tenth on the original y scale, so overfitting costs it directly. R² (y) scores back-transformed median predictions against the raw y, exactly as in the lesson.

      Checks apply the lesson's two screens to each model's residuals on its fitting scale: a partial F-test for leftover curvature (squared and cubed fitted terms) and a test of whether the absolute residuals trend with the fitted value. A model that wins on AICc but fails a check is fitting the wrong shape or the wrong error structure.

      Model families tried
      • Ladder of powers: all 64 combinations of \(x^{p}\) and \(y^{q}\) for \(p, q \in \{-2, -1, -\tfrac12, 0, \tfrac12, 1, 2, 3\}\), which include linear, exponential, power law, logarithmic, reciprocal and Hall–Petch forms.
      • Logit of proportions or percentages against \(x\) and \(\ln x\).
      • Arrhenius \(\ln y\) and \(y\) against \(1/T\) in kelvin, when x is a temperature.
      • Box–Cox power on y estimated by maximum likelihood, against five x rungs.
      • Box–Tidwell \(y = a + b\,x^{\alpha}\) with \(\alpha\) estimated.
      • Polynomials (quadratic and cubic).
      • Nonlinear least squares: exponential and power law with additive error, exponential approach to a floor or ceiling, and the hyperbolic-tangent transition curve.
      • GLMs with a log link: Gamma for positive skewed y and Poisson for counts, against \(x\) and \(\ln x\).
      Use judgement. The best statistical fit is not automatically the right model. Prefer a model whose form matches the physics, check it on new data, and do not extrapolate a cubic or a back-transformed exponential far beyond the observed range.
