1 · Quick Start

The tool has twelve workspaces along the top. Guided Analysis opens first and walks you from a dataset to an analysis and next steps:

  • Guided Analysis — import Excel/CSV or paste a table, reuse datasets, select a question, inspect assumptions and exclusions, and download a reproducible report. Start with a worked example and select Run guided analysis.
  • Scientific Calculator — a full natural-display calculator that operates like a Casio fx-300ES PLUS 2: you type into stacked fractions, radicals, and exponents, with a SHIFT/ALPHA keypad, memory registers, and a history tape.
  • Distributions — probability calculator for 16 distributions with live shaded-region plots, critical values, and reliability metrics.
  • Distribution Lookup Tables — browse the exam reference tables, enter lookup keys and highlight the matching row, column and cell. See section 25 for tail conventions and examples.
  • Hypothesis Tests — ten test presets that compute the statistic, p-value, and decision, and draw the rejection region.
  • Data & Quality — raw-data summaries, regression, capability, I-MR, ANOVA and chi-square.
  • Advanced SPC — choose a chart for your sampling design, establish a baseline, monitor later observations and review capability intervals where supported. See section 23.
  • Cost & Savings — review COQ/COPQ, compare costs at a common production mix, evaluate cash scenarios and track documented benefits. See section 24.
  • Graphing — function, parametric, polar and sequence plots, tables and numerical calculus.
  • Lists & Matrices — advanced expressions, complex numbers, units and a supported-function catalog.
  • Finance — TVM and amortization, with NPV/IRR in the math workspace.
  • Programs — bounded calculation programs using the documented command subset.

3 · Typing Equations — the Natural Display Editor

The equation area is a structural editor: the math on screen is the input itself, not a preview. You can drive it entirely from the keypad, entirely from your keyboard, or mix both.

Creating structures

You pressWhat happens
/ (keyboard) or □⁄□Creates a fraction. The term you just typed jumps up onto the bar as the numerator (parenthesized groups are collected whole), and the cursor lands in the denominator.
^ (keyboard) or x■Opens a raised exponent box. x² / x³ / x⁻¹ insert the filled power and return the cursor to the baseline.
√□Inserts a radical; the sign stretches automatically over anything you build inside — even fraction-in-fraction stacks. SHIFT+√□ gives ∛; SHIFT+x■ gives the n-th root with an index slot.
Letters then (Typing sin tan ln logb ncr… followed by ( converts the letters into a live function with the cursor inside.

Moving around

  • ◀ ▶ walk through the structure — into a radical, across the radicand, out the other side.
  • ▲ ▼ hop between numerator and denominator, and between a root's index and radicand. ▼ also exits an exponent.
  • Click anywhere — on a character (cursor lands before/after depending on which half you click), on an empty dashed slot, or on a slot's padding — the cursor goes exactly there.
  • Backspace / DEL deletes leftward; at the edge of a structure it steps inside; deleting a fraction bar dissolves the structure but keeps its contents.
  • Ctrl+Z undoes (up to 80 steps). Ctrl+C with nothing selected copies the linear form. Pasting a linear expression like sqrt(10.39/(0.25*tan(720/(2*6))*6)) converts it into the stack automatically.
  • Enter = evaluate.

Keyboard / builds a stacked fraction; the keypad ÷ inserts a linear ÷ sign — exactly the distinction the real Casio makes between the fraction key and the divide key. Both compute identically.

4 · The Keypad & SHIFT / ALPHA System

The keypad mirrors the fx-300ES PLUS 2 faceplate. Every key can carry up to three functions:

  • Main label (colour follows the selected theme) — what the key does when pressed alone.
  • Gold legend above-left — the SHIFT function.
  • Red legend above-right — the ALPHA function (mostly the A–F registers).

SHIFT and ALPHA are one-shot prefix keys: press one (its badge lights and the key outlines), press the target key, and the modifier clears itself. Pressing it again cancels. hyp works the same way as a prefix for the trig keys: hyp → sin gives sinh, and hyp → SHIFT → tan gives tanh⁻¹.

The circular replay D-pad navigates the equation (◀▶ through the structure, ▲▼ between stacked levels). On an empty screen, ▲ recalls your previous calculation for editing — the classic Casio replay.

7 · Behavior After Pressing =

The Scientific Calculator follows these entry rules:

  • Digit or function → starts a fresh calculation.
  • Operator (+ − × ÷ ^ % !) → continues from Ans: after 2+2=, pressing +3= gives 7.
  • Fraction key → the answer becomes the numerator.
  • ◀ or click → edit the expression you just evaluated.
  • ▲ on an empty screen → replay: recalls the last entry.

9 · History Tape & STAT Data Panel

History tape (right panel): every evaluated calculation is kept, rendered as a mini stacked equation with its result, and persists between sessions on the website. Click any entry to reload it into the editor for modification. Ans always holds the most recent result.

STAT panel (below the tape, or SHIFT+1): paste data — one value per line or comma-separated — and press Compute stats for n, x̄, s (sample), σ (population), Σx, Σx², min / Q1 / median / Q3 / max. Select X,Y pairs explicitly and paste at least three pairs (one pair per line) for linear regression: slope b, intercept a, r, and r² — the calculator's equivalent of the Casio STAT mode, with paste instead of one-at-a-time entry.

10 · Distributions Tab

Pick a distribution, set its parameters, and ask either question:

  • Given x → probability — enter a value; get the PDF/PMF, left tail P(X ≤ x), right tail P(X ≥ x), the equal-tail two-tailed probability, or P(a ≤ X ≤ b) in Between mode.
  • Given probability → x — enter α or a central probability; get critical values (z₀.₀₂₅ = 1.959964, t-table values, χ² and F criticals) or a central interval.

The plot shades the selected region in amber, labels the boundary values on the axis, prints the shaded probability, and marks the mean with a dashed teal line. Discrete distributions (Binomial, Poisson, Geometric, Negative Binomial, Hypergeometric) draw as bars with the included bars shaded — and the right tail is computed inclusively, P(X ≥ k), as it should be for counts.

Discrete x inputs must be integers; distribution count parameters must also be integers and satisfy their domain. Uniform bounds require lower < upper. Two-tail mode is disabled for discrete distributions because equal tail regions cannot generally match the requested probability. In inverse discrete mode, the selected quantile is the smallest integer with CDF at least the target; the actual inclusive region probability is displayed separately and may differ from the requested value.

Reliability distributions

For Exponential, Weibull, Lognormal, and Gamma, the results panel adds R(t) survival probability, hazard rate h(t), and MTBF. Weibull results include the β interpretation (β < 1 infant mortality, β = 1 random failures, β > 1 wear-out) plus B10 life, and a reminder that F(η) = 63.2% always.

11 · Hypothesis Tests Tab

Ten presets: 1- and 2-sample z (known population standard deviations), 1-sample t, 2-sample t (Welch and pooled), paired t, 1- and 2-proportion z, χ² for one variance, and F for two variances. Enter the summary statistics, choose the alternative (≠, <, >) and α, and run. You get the test statistic, p-value, critical value(s), and a plain-language decision — plus a plot of the reference distribution with the rejection region shaded and your statistic drawn as a red line. If the red line lands in the amber region, the result is significant at your α.

All fields are checked before calculation. Sample sizes and success counts must be whole numbers, standard deviations positive, and success counts no larger than their sample sizes. Changing a field, test or alternative clears previous results. Two-sided (1 − α) intervals accompany the tests, even when the selected hypothesis test is one-sided. Proportion intervals use the Wald approximation and can perform poorly near 0 or 1; sparse expected counts trigger a normal-approximation caution.

For the asymmetric χ² and F distributions, two-tailed p-values use the equal-tail-probability convention, matching Minitab.

Example — did the process mean shift? x̄ = 52, s = 4, n = 25, target μ₀ = 50
Test: 1-sample t · Alternative: ≠ · α = 0.05
t = 2.50, df = 24, p = 0.0197 < 0.05 → Reject H₀ — the statistic falls in the shaded region beyond t₀.₀₂₅,₂₄ = ±2.0639

13 · Accuracy Notes

The legacy distribution engine uses incomplete gamma/beta functions and numerical inversion. Tiny upper tails are evaluated directly where supported. New numerical and interface tests cover known matrix, probability, calculus, finance, control-chart, regression and ANOVA cases. This is targeted validation, not a guarantee of uniform precision across every input. IEEE double precision and displayed rounding can affect extreme cases; a numerical failure or undefined statistic must not be treated as evidence about a process.

14 · Data & Quality

Choose an analysis before pasting data. Load example supplies a complete working dataset. Analyze data replaces the results; changing data or settings invalidates the previous results and disables exports.

AnalysisInput formatOutputs and interpretation
Descriptive statisticsAny arrangement of numeric cells, combined into one sample; minimum two values.Mean, sample and population standard deviations, standard error, quartiles, sum, sum of squares, t confidence interval, histogram, box plot and normal probability plot. Quartiles use linear interpolation (Excel PERCENTILE.INC).
Linear regressionExactly X,Y per row; at least three pairs.Slope, intercept, Pearson r, R², residual standard error (n − 2 denominator), RMSE (n denominator), two-sided slope p-value, slope interval, fitted values and residual plot. Constant X is rejected; constant Y makes r and R² undefined.
Quadratic, cubic, quarticX,Y pairs; at least degree + 2 observations and sufficient distinct X values.Coefficients are displayed in original X units. Calculation uses centered and scaled X; ill-conditioned inputs are rejected. A line connects fitted values at supplied X values.
Logarithmic, exponential, powerX,Y pairs. Logarithmic/power require positive X; exponential/power require positive Y.Logarithmic: a + b ln(X). Exponential: a exp(bX). Power: aXᵇ. Exponential/power fit ln(Y), without retransformation bias correction. R² and RMSE are reported on the original Y scale.
One-way ANOVAOne group per row; at least two groups and two values per group. Unequal group sizes are supported.Classical equal-variance F test, ANOVA table, group summaries and eta-squared. A significant result means at least one mean differs; it does not identify the differing pairs.
Chi-square independenceA rectangular table of nonnegative whole counts, at least 2 × 2. Exclude row/column totals.Pearson χ², approximate p-value, df, Cramér’s V, expected frequencies and cell contributions. No Yates correction. Expected counts below 5 trigger a caution.
Chi-square goodness of fitExactly two rows: observed counts, then expected counts. Positive expected counts; equal totals.Pearson χ² and df = categories − 1. Expected proportions must be specified independently. Adjusted df for parameters estimated from these data are not implemented.

Numbers may be separated by tabs, commas, semicolons or spaces. Use a period for the decimal point and omit headers, units, thousands separators and totals. Scientific notation such as 1.2e-3 is accepted. Blank lines are ignored, but empty cells and malformed values are rejected with their row and cell number. Data entry is limited to 10,000 values, 250,000 characters and magnitude 10¹⁰⁰.

One-way ANOVA worked example
6.9,5.4,5.8,4.6,4.0
8.3,6.8,7.8,9.2,6.5
8.0,10.5,8.1,6.9,9.3
Between SS = 27.8973333; within SS = 17.452; F(2,12) = 9.5911070; p = 0.00324822. At α = 0.05, reject equal population means.

Assumptions: mean intervals and regression inference require independent observations with suitable distributional assumptions. ANOVA assumes approximately normal errors and common population variance. Chi-square tests require independent counts in mutually exclusive categories. A large p-value does not prove equality or independence.

15 · Capability & I-MR Charts

  1. Choose Capability & I-MR, then paste individual measurements in time order.
  2. Enter the lower and/or upper engineering specification. A two-sided specification requires LSL < USL.
  3. Review the I-MR plots and flagged observation numbers before interpreting capability.
  4. Compare observed out-of-specification counts with the separate normal-model estimate.
MeasureCalculation / meaning
Overall sSample standard deviation, using n − 1.
Within sigmaMean absolute difference between consecutive observations divided by 1.128.
Pp / PpkSpread / centering relative to specifications, using overall s.
Cp / CpkSpread / centering relative to specifications, using the moving-range sigma estimate.
Spread index(USL − LSL) ÷ (6 × sigma); requires two limits.
Centering indexSmaller of (USL − mean) ÷ (3 × sigma) and (mean − LSL) ÷ (3 × sigma). For one limit, use the available side.
I-chart limitsMean ± 3 × within sigma.
MR-chart limitsLower 0; center MR̄; upper 3.267 × MR̄.

Only points beyond the limits are flagged. Other run rules are not tested. No flags does not prove stability. The same data estimate the limits and populate the chart. Autocorrelation, mixed populations, small samples and non-normality can invalidate capability interpretations. Specification limits are engineering requirements; control limits describe process behavior. Values exactly on specification limits count as conforming.

Normal-model PPM uses the sample mean and overall s without a 1.5-sigma shift. It is not the observed defect rate. Zero variation is rejected; a very small sample cannot justify a confident capability claim.

NIST flow-rate example
49.6,47.6,49.9,51.3,47.8,51.2,52.6,52.4,53.6,52.1
Mean 50.81; MR̄ 1.8777778; I-chart limits 45.8159102 and 55.8040898. This example contains only ten observations and is for learning the calculation.

This workspace uses a locally served copy of math.js 15.2.0. It runs in a separate worker. Use the searchable supported-function catalog and the worked-example menu. RAD/DEG applies to sin/cos/tan, sec/csc/cot, their inverse functions, atan2, and the string-based nDeriv/fnInt/solve/seq helpers. Complex arg/angle always returns radians. Derivatives and integrals use the selected angle unit for the independent variable; for example, nDeriv("sin(x)",0) in DEG is π/180, and fnInt("sin(x)",0,180) is 360/π. Scientific Calculator angle mode is separate. Use parentheses and explicit operators when porting an expression from another calculator.

Object access, matrix/list indexing and user-defined function declarations are disabled. Whole-array operations, assignments and supported functions remain available. Arrays are limited to 10,000 cells; calculations time out after six seconds, resetting shared variables. Input is limited to 8,000 characters per evaluation. A calculation tape can be downloaded as text. Ans contains the last result, including the final result of a multi-line evaluation. Editing graph/table/program inputs clears the corresponding old output. Evaluating math expressions, running programs or clearing/resetting shared variables invalidates existing graphs so they can be redrawn from the current values; the calculation tape intentionally retains earlier calculations.

18 · Finance & Amortization

  1. Select the unknown: N, annual nominal I%, PV, PMT or FV. Its input is disabled and ignored.
  2. Enter the known values, payments per year, compounds per year, and beginning/end payment timing.
  3. Use positive amounts for cash received and negative amounts for cash paid.
  4. Press Solve TVM. For a positive principal and negative payment with an integer N ≤ 600, an amortization table is also displayed.

N counts payments, not years. If r is the effective rate per payment period, r = (1 + I% ÷ (100 × C/Y))^(C/Y ÷ P/Y) − 1. The solver uses PV(1+r)ᴺ + PMT × annuity factor + FV = 0, with the payment factor multiplied by (1+r) for beginning-of-period payments. At zero interest, the annuity factor is N.

Five-year loan example
N = 60, annual I% = 6, PV = 20,000, FV = 0, P/Y = C/Y = 12, end-of-period payments → PMT ≈ −386.656030589.

In Lists & Matrices, npv(0.1,[-1000,400,400,400]) gives −5.25920360631. The first cash flow is at time zero; the rate is a decimal per period. irr([-1000,400,400,400],0,1) finds a periodic IRR of about 0.0970102574 within the bracket [0,1]. IRR may have multiple solutions or none. The TVM rate search starts just above the compounding-domain boundary of −100 × C/Y percent (with a small numerical margin) and expands its positive upper bound up to 40 times. It requires a sign-changing bracket and can fail for non-unique solutions or numerically extreme inputs. N is bracketed from 0 to 100,000; valid solutions outside that range are not found. Unbracketed or non-unique all-zero cash flows are rejected. PV/PMT/FV are solved directly; bracketed rate/N searches use scaled cash flows to avoid overflow.

eff(6,12) returns the annual effective percentage for 6% nominal compounded monthly. nom(effectivePercent,compoundsPerYear) reverses the conversion. Amortization uses unrounded internal cash flows; lender rounding, fees and payment conventions can differ.

19 · Calculation Programs

The program editor supports a deliberately limited, documented command language. It is not a complete TI-BASIC interpreter and does not load tokenized calculator files. Programs share session variables with the math workspace. Download source preserves the text for later reuse.

0→S
For(I,1,10)
  S+I→S
End
Disp S

This example prints 55. Supported commands are assignments with →, -> or =; Disp expression; ClrHome; For; While; If / Then / Else / End; and Stop. Every control block needs End. Conditions support comparisons and Boolean operators; a single = in an If/While condition means equality. Maximum: 500 lines, 10,000 execution steps and 1,000 output lines, plus the worker time limit.

Not supported: Goto/Lbl, interactive Input/Prompt/Menu, key scanning, drawing commands, list indexing, strings with TI-specific token semantics, assembly, hardware calls, .8xp imports, or installed TI applications. Unsupported statements produce errors; they are not silently skipped.

Limits and signal rules

Individuals use MR-bar/1.128 to estimate within variation. X-bar/R uses R-bar/d2; X-bar/S uses S-bar/c4. The paired spread chart has a zero-truncated lower limit. Attribute centers use total counts divided by total inspected units or exposure; p/u limits change with each row's denominator. These are symmetric three-sigma limits, truncated to the possible range; exact count-tail probabilities and dispersion corrections are not implemented.

EWMA uses λ (default 0.2) and L (default 3), with startup limits that approach their steady-state width. CUSUM uses standardized deviations with k = 0.5 and h = 5 by default; upper and lower accumulations are shown as positive magnitudes. These tuning choices are editable and change sensitivity; they are not calibrated average-run-length guarantees for an estimated baseline.

EWMA startup limits use a cancellation-resistant calculation for small λ. If the limits cannot be resolved numerically, analysis stops instead of publishing a zero-width band or false signal. Review λ and the measurement scale; changing λ changes the chart's statistical sensitivity, not just its display.

Every chart flags values strictly outside its limits. Optional mean-chart rules for Individuals, X-bar/R and X-bar/S flag the endpoint of nine successive points strictly on one side of the center, or six strictly increasing/decreasing points. Ties break the relevant pattern; rule windows do not cross phases. These are selected rules, not a complete Nelson or Western Electric rule suite. More rules increase false alarms. Attribute and sequential charts do not use these extra mean-chart rules.

Stability is different from specification compliance

A process can be stable and still produce unacceptable material; it can also meet today's specifications while shifting unpredictably. Control limits describe behavior. Specification limits define the engineering/customer requirement. This workspace does not overlay individual specifications on subgroup-mean or EWMA statistics, where the comparison would be misleading.

Optional LSL/USL apply to individual baseline and monitoring measurements. Observed below/above counts are reported separately; values exactly on a specification boundary count as within specification. These descriptive counts remain available without a normality assumption. No normal-model future defect forecast is made.

Capability indices require an explicit acknowledgement of a representative, stable, approximately normal process and an adequate measurement system. The tool withholds indices and intervals if the selected baseline charts/rules detect signals. Absence of a signal is not proof that the model is valid; investigate autocorrelation, mixtures and non-normality independently.

Events, Pareto and comparability

Events is a nonnegative whole-number count allocated to that cost line. It is independent of Quantity. When a single incident has several cost lines, allocate its count once or use mutually exclusive event definitions; otherwise the frequency Pareto will double-count it. Training and inspection lines may have zero events. Cost and frequency rankings group by category plus cause and are sorted separately, with cumulative shares. The screen shows the top twelve causes; report/CSV include the complete ranking. Zero totals have no percentage share.

Enter the baseline rationale, valuation basis, volume unit, product-mix treatment and potential confounders in the assumptions field. Confirm complete comparable records before analysis. Missing cost rows are treated as zero only under this explicit acknowledgement. Identical cost rows are retained with a warning; legitimate repeated transactions are not silently removed.

Volume and product-mix adjustment

For each product/category, the baseline cost rate is baseline cost divided by baseline volume. Multiply that rate by the comparison-period volume for the same product, then sum across products. The result is the expected cost at baseline rates for the current volume and mix. Adjusted reduction = expected cost − actual comparison cost. Negative results indicate higher cost than that benchmark.

The period table reports COQ, COPQ, COQ per production unit, COQ as a share of revenue, and adjusted COQ reduction. Revenue share is unavailable if any product revenue is unknown or total revenue is zero. A product absent from the baseline makes the adjusted comparison unavailable rather than assigning it a zero baseline rate. Product-mix details show volume, share and baseline COPQ per unit.

This is a rate-based benchmark, not a causal model. Fixed prevention/appraisal costs may not scale with volume; review that assumption. Changes in prices, product specifications, accounting allocation, utilization, reporting completeness or measurement practice can explain an apparent reduction. Normalize those effects separately or document their treatment before attributing benefits to the project.

Projected improvement business case

The forecast form is independent of the observed cost comparison and the benefit register. Enter an annual gross cash-savings forecast, separate annual cost avoidance and released-capacity valuations, initial implementation cash cost, annual incremental cash operating cost, a 1–30-year horizon and a 0–100% annual discount rate. Do not re-enter an operating cost already deducted from a net savings forecast.

Cash net flow each year = gross cash savings − incremental operating cash cost. Year 0 contains the implementation outlay. NPV discounts each subsequent net flow at year-end and subtracts the initial cost. Cash ROI = total undiscounted net benefit divided by total implementation plus operating cash cost; it is unavailable when total cost is zero. Simple payback assumes uniform within-year cash accrual and is shown only if the initial outlay is recovered within the selected horizon. This payback convention differs from the year-end timing used for NPV.

The low/base/high scenarios vary gross annual cash savings by the chosen ±0–100% sensitivity while keeping costs fixed. They are scenarios, not probabilities or confidence intervals. Cost avoidance and capacity valuations are displayed separately and do not enter cash NPV, ROI or payback. This model has no automatic inflation, tax, financing, ramp-up, terminal-value or discount-rate recommendation. Use a discount rate consistent with the cash-flow basis. For custom cash-flow timing, use the existing Finance or Lists & Matrices workspace.

Cost analysis rejects non-finite derived results and positive cost lines that underflow to zero. Check tiny production volumes, revenues or project costs, and use consistent, practical currency and production units. A failed calculation clears prior results and disables result downloads; it does not export infinity as a valid metric or silently replace it with a missing JSON value. Legitimately unavailable metrics, such as ROI with zero total cost, remain explicitly unavailable.

Track actuals and record verification

Register types are Cash savings, Cost avoidance, Released capacity, Implementation cost and Recurring cost. Capacity Amount is a monetary valuation; record the released quantity, unit and valuation basis in Notes. Negative benefit amounts can record reversals or adverse effects. Implementation/recurring costs must be nonnegative. Cost-ledger quantity/rates are also nonnegative; reconcile credits externally into an appropriate nonnegative net line and explain the basis.

Statuses are Projected, Realized and Verified. Update the same ID when it advances; do not create one row for each stage. Projected rows may use a future period label; actual rows must reference a period in the production table. Verified records require Owner, Evidence, a valid nonfuture YYYY-MM-DD Date and Notes explaining the verification/basis. Evidence references are retained as text; the tool does not open documents, authenticate owners or approve claims.

Actual recorded totals include Realized plus Verified once. Verified is a subset, not an additional benefit. Project-wide net cash = cash savings − implementation cost − recurring cost within the relevant status scope. Totals include baseline-period initial project costs. Missing or unverified costs can overstate a verified subtotal, so pending costs are highlighted. Period tracking separates actual cash benefits, verified cash benefits, verified project costs and pending entries.

The forecast form and projected register are separate planning views and are not combined. Likewise, do not add normalized cost reductions to registered cash benefits: they may describe the same underlying improvement. Reconcile benefit ownership, scope, accounting evidence, attribution and costs with the responsible finance reviewer.

Equations and notation

Equations are authored in LaTeX and displayed as native mathematical notation, including fractions, roots, subscripts and quantiles. The original LaTeX is retained in each equation’s MathML annotation. Programming examples elsewhere use literal input syntax so they can be copied into the calculator.

Standard normal

Φ(z)=Pr⁡(Z≤z)\Phi(z)=\Pr(Z\le z)Pr⁡(Z>z)=1−Φ(z)\Pr(Z>z)=1-\Phi(z)

The standard-normal cumulative distribution gives the area to the left of the entered value.

Student’s t

Pr⁡(Tν>tα,ν)=α\Pr(T_\nu>t_{\alpha,\nu})=\alpha

The subscript alpha denotes the upper-tail probability; nu is the degrees of freedom.

Chi-square

Pr⁡(χν2>cα,ν)=α\Pr(\chi^2_\nu>c_{\alpha,\nu})=\alpha

Critical values use upper-tail probability alpha and degrees of freedom nu.

F distribution

Pr⁡(Fν1,ν2>fα;ν1,ν2)=α\Pr(F_{\nu_1,\nu_2}>f_{\alpha;\nu_1,\nu_2})=\alpha

The numerator degrees of freedom come first; the denominator degrees of freedom come second.

Binomial PMF

Pr⁡(X=x)=(nx)px(1−p)n−x\Pr(X=x)=\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}

Independent trials with constant success probability; the count ranges from zero through n.

Binomial cumulative

Pr⁡(X≤x)=∑j=0x(nj)pj(1−p)n−j\Pr(X\le x)=\sum_{j=0}^{x}\binom{n}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-j}

The cumulative probability includes the entered count.

Poisson PMF

Pr⁡(X=x)=e−λλxx!\Pr(X=x)=\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}

Lambda is the expected count in the observation interval.

Poisson cumulative

Pr⁡(X≤x)=e−λ∑j=0xλjj!\Pr(X\le x)=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{x}\frac{\lambda^j}{j!}

The cumulative probability includes the entered count.

Exponential

X=λt=tμX=\lambda t=\frac{t}{\mu}F(X)=1−e−XF(X)=1-e^{-X}S(X)=e−XS(X)=e^{-X}

Standardized time is rate times elapsed time; mu is mean lifetime. F is failure probability and S is survival probability.

Tukey studentized range

Pr⁡(Qk,ν>qα;k,ν)=α\Pr(Q_{k,\nu}>q_{\alpha;k,\nu})=\alpha

Tukey critical values use the number of groups and error degrees of freedom.

Duncan multiple range

αp=1−(1−α)p−1\alpha_p=1-(1-\alpha)^{p-1}SSR⁡(p,ν,α)=FQp,ν−1(1−αp)\operatorname{SSR}(p,\nu,\alpha)=F^{-1}_{Q_{p,\nu}}(1-\alpha_p)

Duncan uses the number of ordered means spanned and a range-dependent significance level.

Control-chart constants

A2=3d2nA_2=\frac{3}{d_2\sqrt n}A3=3c4nA_3=\frac{3}{c_4\sqrt n}UCL⁡Xˉ,LCL⁡Xˉ=Xˉˉ±A2Rˉ\operatorname{UCL}_{\bar X},\operatorname{LCL}_{\bar X}=\bar{\bar X}\pm A_2\bar R

Factors depend on subgroup size. The displayed mean limits apply to an X-bar/R chart.

Sigma level / DPMO

PPM⁡centered=2×106[1−Φ(z)]\operatorname{PPM}_{\mathrm{centered}}=2\times10^6[1-\Phi(z)]DPMO⁡1.5σ=106[Φ(1.5−z)+1−Φ(z+1.5)]\operatorname{DPMO}_{1.5\sigma}=10^6[\Phi(1.5-z)+1-\Phi(z+1.5)]

The shifted convention includes both tails. Centered PPM is rounded to a whole number; shifted PPM to one decimal.

Median ranks

MR⁡(i,n)=i−0.3n+0.4\operatorname{MR}(i,n)=\frac{i-0.3}{n+0.4}

Bernard’s approximation for complete, ordered failure data; this does not handle censoring.

Normal scores

NS⁡(i,n)=Φ−1 ⁣(i−0.375n+0.25)\operatorname{NS}(i,n)=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{i-0.375}{n+0.25}\right)

Blom’s plotting-position approximation transformed by the standard-normal inverse CDF.

One-sided normal tolerance factors

k=tν,δ−1(γ)nk=\frac{t^{-1}_{\nu,\delta}(\gamma)}{\sqrt n}ν=n−1,δ=zPn\nu=n-1,\quad\delta=z_P\sqrt nzP=Φ−1(P)z_P=\Phi^{-1}(P)UB⁡=xˉ+ks,LB⁡=xˉ−ks\operatorname{UB}=\bar x+ks,\quad\operatorname{LB}=\bar x-ks

The inverse is the noncentral t quantile at confidence gamma, with degrees of freedom nu and noncentrality delta. P is minimum population coverage. Each bound is one-sided; combining them does not give the corresponding two-sided tolerance interval.
